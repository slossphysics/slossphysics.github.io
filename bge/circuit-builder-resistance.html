Circuit builder: resistance

LI: I am learning what resistance does to the current in a circuit.

SC: I can build series and parallel circuits · I can use a resistor to make a lamp dimmer · I can use a variable resistor to change the current.

15:00
Tasks

1.Build a circuit that makes a lamp light.

Hint

Current needs a complete loop: from one end of the cell, through the lamp, and back to the other end of the cell.

The lamp lights because there is a complete circuit: one unbroken loop from the cell, through the lamp and back.

2.Add a switch so that you can turn the lamp on and off. Tap the switch to test it.

Hint

The switch has to be in the same loop as the lamp. A new switch starts open.

An open switch makes a gap in the loop, so no current can flow anywhere in that loop.

3.Build a circuit with one cell and two lamps in series. Are the lamps brighter or dimmer than one lamp on its own?

Hint

Series means there is only one loop. The current goes through one lamp and then the other.

In series the lamps are dimmer than one lamp on its own: the cell has to push the current through both. Take one lamp out and the other goes off too.

4.Build a circuit with one cell and two lamps in parallel. How bright are they this time?

Hint

Parallel means each lamp has its own branch. Both ends of each lamp are joined to the cell.

In parallel each lamp is as bright as one lamp on its own, and if one is taken out the other stays on. That is why the lights in a house are wired in parallel.

5.Press Clear. Light one lamp with one cell, then add a resistor to the same loop. What happens to the lamp?

Hint

Drop the resistor onto one of the wires and it joins into the loop.

The lamp gets dimmer. The resistor makes it harder for current to flow, so the current is smaller.

6.Delete the resistor and put a variable resistor in its place. Tap it and move its slider. Make the lamp as bright as you can, then as dim as you can.

Hint

Tap the resistor and press Delete. Then drop a variable resistor onto the wire. When you tap it, a slider appears.

The lamp is brightest when the resistance is smallest (0 Ω) and dimmest when the resistance is biggest (20 Ω). This is how a dimmer switch works.

7.Add an ammeter to the loop. In your jotter, write the current when the resistance is smallest and when it is biggest.

Hint

An ammeter goes in series: drop it onto a wire and it joins into the loop.

At 0 Ω the ammeter reads 0.30 A. At 20 Ω it reads 0.06 A. When the resistance goes up, the current goes down.

Challenge8.Use one cell, two lamps and a variable resistor. Build a circuit where the variable resistor dims one lamp and leaves the other lamp bright.

Put the lamps in parallel, with the variable resistor in one lamp's branch. It only changes the current in its own branch, so the other lamp stays just as bright.

Extra9.Press Clear. Build one loop with a cell, a lamp, a variable resistor and an ammeter. Slide the variable resistor until the ammeter reads exactly 0.10 A. Write the resistance in your jotter.

Hint

Look at the reading. If the current is too big, the resistance needs to change. Which way?

10 Ω gives 0.10 A. That is the same resistance as the fixed resistor in task 5, so the lamp is just as bright as it was then.

Extra10.Press Clear. Build one loop with a cell, a lamp, an ammeter and two resistors in series. With one resistor the current was 0.10 A. Is it bigger or smaller with two?

Hint

Build the loop with one resistor first.

Another hint

Drop the second resistor onto a wire in the same loop, so the current has to go through one resistor and then the other.

The current is smaller: 0.06 A. In series the current has to get through both resistors, so there is more resistance in the loop.

Extra11.Now put the two resistors in parallel with each other, side by side. Keep the lamp and the ammeter in the main loop. First predict: will the current be bigger or smaller than 0.10 A?

Hint

Start with the loop from task 10, then delete one resistor and join the gap with a wire.

Another hint

Put the second resistor on the row below the first one.

Another hint

Join the left end of the new resistor to the left end of the first one, and the right end to the right end. Now the current has two ways through.

The current is bigger: 0.15 A. Two resistors in parallel give the current two paths, so together they have less resistance than one resistor on its own.

Short circuit! The current has a path with almost no resistance, so it is far too big. A real cell would get hot and go flat quickly.

  • Add a wire: drag from the end of a component to where the wire should go. Or tap the end, then tap where it should go.
  • Move, turn or delete: drag a component to move it. Tap it for Turn and Delete, or drag it off the grid.
  • Variable resistor: tap it, then drag the slider to change its resistance. A bigger resistance means a smaller current.
  • Switches: tap a switch to open or close it.
  • The moving dots show the electrons, which flow from the negative end of the cell (the short line) round to the positive end. Faster dots mean a bigger current. Show conventional flow turns the dots round, positive to negative: the direction used for current in later courses.
