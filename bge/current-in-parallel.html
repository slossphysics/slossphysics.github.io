Experiment: current in parallel

LI: I am learning the rule for current in a parallel circuit.

SC: I can add a branch to a circuit · I can measure the current from the cell and in each branch · I can state the rule for current in a parallel circuit.

Steps

1.This circuit has one lamp, so there is only one path for the current. Tap the switch to close it. Write the readings on A1 and A2 in the first row of your table.

The lamp is lit. Write the reading on A1, then the reading on A2, in the first row of your table.

2.Add a second lamp in a branch of its own, so it is in parallel with lamp 1. Put an ammeter in the new branch too.

Hint

Drag a lamp into the space below lamp 1. Draw a wire from each end of it to the long wires at the sides. Then drop an ammeter onto the wire beside your new lamp.

Two lamps in parallel. In the second row of your table, write the reading on A1, then the reading on the ammeter in each branch.

3.Add a third lamp in another branch of its own, with its own ammeter.

Hint

Build it the same way as the second branch, in the space below it: a lamp, a wire from each end to the long wires at the sides, then an ammeter dropped onto the new wire.

Three lamps in parallel. In the third row, write the reading on A1, then the reading in each branch. Your table is now complete: scroll down to the Conclusion.

Challenge4.A resistor lets less current through than a lamp. Predict what A1 will read with a resistor in a fourth branch. Then add one, with its own ammeter, to test your prediction.

Hint

Keep all three lamps. Build the resistor's branch in the space below them, the same way as the others.

The resistor’s branch carries 0.15 A and A1 goes up by exactly that, to 1.05 A. The branches do not have to carry the same current, but A1 always reads the branch currents added together.

Short circuit! The current has a path with almost no resistance, so it is far too big. A real cell would get hot and go flat quickly.

  • Add a lamp: drag it onto the grid, or tap it.
  • Add a wire: drag from the end of a component to where the wire should go. Or tap the end, then tap where it should go.
  • Add an ammeter: drop it onto a straight part of a wire and it joins in. Or tap the wire, then tap Ammeter.
  • Change your mind: tap a part or wire you added for Delete, or use Undo. The parts that were there at the start are fixed in place.
  • The moving dots show the current. Faster dots mean a bigger current. Watch what they do where the wire splits.

Conclusion

Answer these when your results table is complete.

5.Look at each row of your table. How does the current from the cell (A1) compare with the currents in the branches?

Hint

In your last row, add up the three branch readings. Compare your total with the reading on A1.

With three lamps, each branch carries 0.30 A and A1 reads 0.90 A: 0.30 + 0.30 + 0.30 = 0.90. The current from the cell is the branch currents added together.

6.Finish the rule for current in a parallel circuit. Two of the words are not needed.

Drag each word into its gap, or tap a word and then the gap.

In a parallel circuit, the current from the cell between the branches.

The current from the cell is equal to the of the currents in the branches.

Write this rule in your jotter: In a parallel circuit, the current from the cell splits between the branches. The current from the cell is equal to the sum of the currents in the branches.
In your real experiment the readings may not have added up exactly. Real wires, meters and connections all have a little resistance of their own, which changes the readings slightly. This experiment shows what happens without that.
