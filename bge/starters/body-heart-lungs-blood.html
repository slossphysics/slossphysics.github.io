Heart, lungs and blood

LI: I am learning how the respiratory and circulatory systems get oxygen to every cell.

SC: I can name the parts of the lungs · I can describe what happens in gas exchange · I can say what blood carries · I can give lifestyle risks for the heart and lungs.

5:00

1.Match each name to its number on the diagram of the lungs.

Drag each name onto its number, or tap a name and then a number.

Air goes down the windpipe, which splits into two bronchi (one for each lung). These branch again and again inside the lungs, ending in tiny air sacs.

2.In the air sacs of the lungs, which gas passes from the air into the blood?

Oxygen passes from the air sacs into the blood. Carbon dioxide passes from the blood into the air sacs and is breathed out. This swap is called gas exchange.

3.What is the job of the heart?

The heart is a muscle that pumps blood through the blood vessels to every part of the body.

4.Blood carries things to the body's cells and takes things away. Sort these.

Drag each one into its group, or tap it and then the group.

Cells need oxygen and glucose to release energy. They make wastes such as carbon dioxide and urea, which the blood carries away to the lungs and kidneys.

5.Match each blood vessel to what it does.

Drag each job onto a blood vessel, or tap a job and then a vessel.

Hint

A memory trick: arteries carry blood away from the heart.

Arteries carry blood away from the heart and veins bring it back. Capillaries are so thin that oxygen and glucose can pass through their walls into the cells.

6.Tap all the things that raise the risk of heart disease. There are 4.

Smoking, lack of exercise, a high-fat diet and obesity all raise the risk of heart disease and stroke. These are risks we can do something about.

7.Smoking damages the tiny air sacs in the lungs. Why does this make a smoker get out of breath easily?

Gas exchange happens in the air sacs. If they are damaged, less oxygen gets into the blood, so the muscles run short of oxygen and the person gets breathless.

Hint

One beat pumps 0.07 litres. How much would 70 beats pump?

  • volume per minute = volume per beat × beats per minute
  • volume per minute = 0.07 × 70
  • volume per minute = 4.9 litres
That is about all the blood in an adult's body, every minute.

Challenge9.During exercise, both your breathing rate and your heart rate go up. What is the best explanation?

Hint

Think about both systems: what does each one do for a working muscle cell?

Working muscles use up oxygen and glucose faster and make more carbon dioxide. Faster breathing gets more oxygen in and carbon dioxide out. A faster heart rate carries them to and from the muscles more quickly. The two systems work together.
