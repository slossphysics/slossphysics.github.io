BGE · Fourth level · Biological systems · SCN 4-12b

Behaviour and survival

LI: I am learning how animals change their behaviour in response to changes around them, and how this helps them survive.

SC: I can tell learned behaviour from behaviour an animal is born with · I can draw a conclusion from choice chamber results · I can explain how a behaviour helps a species survive.

5:00

1.What is a stimulus?

A stimulus is a change the animal can detect, such as light, temperature, a sound or a smell. What the animal does about it is its response.

2.Sort these behaviours into learned behaviour and behaviour the animal is born with.

Drag each one into its group, or tap it and then the group.

Hint

Ask: could the animal do this the very first time, without any experience? If it needed to find out first, it is learned.

Learned behaviour comes from experience: the animal links a stimulus with what happened before. Behaviour an animal is born with works the first time, with no practice.

3.Ten woodlice are put in the middle of a choice chamber. One half is covered so it is dark. This is where they are after 10 minutes. What can you conclude?

8 out of 10 woodlice ended up on the dark side, so woodlice respond to light by moving towards the dark.

4.Woodlice move towards dark places. Tap all the reasons this helps them survive. There are 2.

Hiding in the dark keeps woodlice out of sight of predators. Dark places are often damp too, and woodlice dry out quickly in dry air.

5.20 woodlice are put in a choice chamber with a damp side and a dry side. After 10 minutes, 15 of them are on the damp side. What percentage of the woodlice chose the damp side?

%

That's the number of woodlice, not the percentage. Divide by the total, then multiply by 100.

That's the percentage on the dry side. The question asks about the damp side.

That's the fraction as a decimal. Multiply by 100 to turn it into a percentage.

  • percentage = number on damp side / total × 100
  • percentage = 15 / 20 × 100
  • percentage = 75 %

6.A pupil wants to test whether woodlice prefer light or dark. Tap all the things they should keep the same on both sides to make it a fair test. There are 2.

Hint

In a fair test you change only one thing. Which thing is being tested here? Everything else that could affect the woodlice must stay the same.

Only the light should be different. If one side were also damper or warmer, you couldn't tell whether the woodlice were choosing the dark, the damp or the warmth.

7.Why should the pupil repeat the choice chamber experiment several times with different woodlice?

A single result could be down to chance. If repeats give a similar result, we can be more confident in the conclusion.

8.A young bird eats a brightly striped caterpillar that tastes horrible. After that, it leaves caterpillars with those stripes alone. How does this learned behaviour help the bird?

The bird links the stripes with a bad taste. Avoiding them saves it from wasting energy or eating something harmful, which helps it survive.

Challenge9.20 woodlice are put in a choice chamber with four sections. This is where they are after 10 minutes. Which stimulus seems to matter more to the woodlice?

Hint

Add up the two damp sections. Then add up the two dark sections. Compare the totals.

Damp sections: 11 + 6 = 17. Dark sections: 11 + 2 = 13. More woodlice chose damp than chose dark, so damp seems to matter more. Even in the light, 6 stayed on the damp side.
