Monitoring health

LI: I am learning how technology is used to monitor health.

SC: I can choose an instrument to measure something about my health · I can work out a pulse rate and a recovery time · I can calculate BMI and say what it can't tell us.

5:00

1.Match each instrument to what it measures.

Drag each instrument onto what it measures, or tap an instrument and then a measurement.

Doctors, nurses and people at home use instruments like these to monitor health and spot problems early.

2.Tap all the places where it is easy to feel your own pulse. There are 2.

A pulse is felt where an artery runs close to the skin over something firm, such as the wrist and the neck. Use two fingers, not your thumb, which has a pulse of its own.

3.A pupil counts 18 pulse beats in 15 seconds. What is their pulse rate in beats per minute?

beats per minute

That is the number of beats in 15 seconds. How many lots of 15 seconds are there in a minute?

You've divided 18 by 15. That gives beats per second, not per minute.

That would be the number of beats in 30 seconds. A minute is 60 seconds.

Hint

How many lots of 15 seconds make one minute?

  • 60 s ÷ 15 s = 4
  • pulse rate = 18 × 4
  • pulse rate = 72 beats per minute

4.What is meant by someone's recovery time after exercise?

Recovery time is how long the pulse takes to return to its resting rate after exercise. The fitter someone is, the shorter it usually is.

A's pulse gets back to resting much faster. A shorter recovery time is a sign of a fitter heart and lungs.

7.Body mass index (BMI) is calculated from a person's mass and height. An adult has a mass of 60 kg and a height of 1.60 m. Calculate their BMI.

kg/m²

You've divided by the height only once. Work out height × height first.

You've divided the wrong way round. It is mass divided by height × height.

You've multiplied. It is mass divided by height × height.

Hint

Work out 1.60 × 1.60 first, then divide the mass by your answer.

  • BMI = mass ÷ (height × height)
  • BMI = 60 ÷ (1.60 × 1.60)
  • BMI = 23.4 kg/m²

8.The NHS invites some adults for screening, for example for bowel cancer. Why?

Screening checks healthy-looking people so that conditions such as bowel cancer, diabetes or eye disease are found early. Early treatment usually works better.

Challenge9.Two adults are both 1.80 m tall and have a mass of 95 kg, so both have a BMI of 29.3. One is a rugby player with lots of muscle. The other does no exercise. What does this show about BMI?

Hint

BMI is worked out from just two measurements. Is there anything about the rugby player's body those two numbers don't show?

BMI uses only mass and height. Muscle is heavy, so a muscly athlete can have a high BMI without being unhealthy. Doctors use BMI alongside other measurements, such as waist size and blood pressure.
