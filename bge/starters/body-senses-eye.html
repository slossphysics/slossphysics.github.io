BGE · Second level · Biological systems · SCN 2-12b

The senses and the eye

LI: I am learning how my senses detect what is around me and keep me safe.

SC: I can match each sense to its organ · I can explain how senses work together · I can describe how the pupil changes in bright and dim light.

5:00

1.Match each sense to its sense organ.

Drag each sense onto its organ, or tap a sense and then an organ.

Each sense organ detects something different: light (eyes), sound (ears), smells (nose), tastes (tongue), and touch, pressure and temperature (skin).

2.Each of these warns you of danger. Match each one to the sense that detects it.

Drag each danger onto a sense, or tap a danger and then a sense.

Our senses warn us about danger so we can react quickly: move away from heat, leave a building, or watch our step.

3.When you have a cold and a blocked nose, food seems to have much less flavour. Why?

Much of what we call flavour comes from smell. With a blocked nose only the taste from the tongue is left, so food seems bland. Senses often work together.

4.Many people who are blind rely more on their hearing and touch, and become very good at using them. What does this show?

When one sense is impaired, people can learn to rely more on the others, for example reading Braille by touch or listening for traffic. A lost sense can also make life harder, so it affects the other senses in both ways.

5.This is the front of an eye. What is the part marked with a question mark called?

The pupil is the hole in the middle of the eye that lets light in. It looks black because the light that goes in doesn't come back out.

6.These two eyes belong to the same person, at different times. Which one is in bright light?

In bright light the pupil gets smaller, so less light gets in and the eye is protected. Eye A is in bright light.

7.Why does the pupil get bigger in dim light?

In dim light there is not much light about, so the pupil gets bigger to let more light in.

8.Match each part of the eye to its job.

Drag each job onto a part, or tap a job and then a part.

Hint

Look at the diagram. Light gets in through the dark centre. The coloured part is all around it.

The iris has muscles that make the pupil bigger or smaller. The eyelid closes to keep out dust and very bright light.

Challenge9.You walk from a sunny playground into a dark room. At first you can hardly see anything, but after a while you can see better. What is happening?

Hint

Start with the sunny playground. What size were your pupils there? What size do they need to be in the dark room?

In the bright playground your pupils were small. In the dark room they let in too little light at first. Over the next few moments they get bigger, letting more light in, so you see better.
