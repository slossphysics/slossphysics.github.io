Cell division and stem cells

LI: I am learning why cells divide and how stem cells can be used to treat disease.

SC: I can explain why cell division is needed for growth and repair · I can describe one medical use of stem cells · I can give arguments for and against using stem cells.

5:00

1.Tap all the reasons our bodies need cells to divide. There are 2.

Cell division makes more cells. We need them to grow and to repair damage, such as healing a cut.

2.A skin cell divides into two new cells. What genetic information do the new cells get?

The genetic information is copied first, so each new cell gets a full, identical set of instructions.

3.Sort these into cell division for growth and cell division for repair.

Drag each one into its group, or tap it and then the group.

In growth, the body or plant gets bigger. In repair, new cells replace damaged ones. Both happen by cell division.

4.One cell divides into 2. Then each new cell divides, and so on. How many cells are there after 5 rounds of division?

You've worked out 5 × 2. But the number of cells doubles every round: 1, 2, 4, …

That's after 4 rounds. Count one more doubling.

That's after 6 rounds. Start from 1 cell and count 5 doublings.

Hint

Write it out: 1 cell, then 2, then 4… Keep doubling until you have done 5 rounds.

  • 1 → 2 → 4 → 8 → 16 → 32
  • 1 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 32 cells

5.What is a stem cell?

A stem cell is unspecialised. It can divide to make more cells, which can then become specialised cells such as blood, muscle or nerve cells.

6.Tap all the places stem cells can be collected from. There are 3.

Stem cells are found in bone marrow, in early embryos, and in umbilical cord blood, which can be collected and stored after a birth.

7.Leukaemia is a cancer of the blood. Some patients are given a bone marrow transplant from a healthy donor. How does this help?

Bone marrow contains stem cells that make blood cells. The donor's healthy stem cells settle in and keep dividing to make new blood cells.

8.Some people support using stem cells from embryos for research, and some are against it. Sort these arguments into "for" and "against".

Drag each one into its group, or tap it and then the group.

There are reasoned arguments on both sides. A good debate uses evidence and correct words, and respects that people weigh these arguments differently.

9.Which of these is a fact, not an opinion?

A fact can be tested and shown to be true. An opinion is a view that people can disagree about. In a debate, use facts as evidence to back up your opinion.

Challenge10.A cut is healing. At the start, there are 50 skin cells along its edge. Every cell divides once a day, and every new cell divides too. How many cells will there be after 4 days?

You've worked out 50 × 4. But the number doubles every day, so it isn't just 4 lots of 50.

That's after 3 days. Double once more.

That's how many cells come from one cell. There were 50 cells at the start.

Hint

The number of cells doubles each day. Start with 50 and double it four times.

  • 50 → 100 → 200 → 400 → 800
  • 50 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 800 cells
