BGE · Third level · Biological systems · SCN 3-13a

Specialised cells

LI: I am learning how some cells have a special shape to do a particular job.

SC: I can recognise five specialised cells · I can match each cell to its job · I can explain how a cell's shape helps it do its job.

5:00

1.What is a specialised cell?

A specialised cell has a special shape or special parts that help it do one job well.

2.Match each drawing to the name of the cell.

Drag each cell onto its name, or tap a cell and then its name.

Hint

Look for a tail, a long thin wire with branches, and a long finger poking out of a box shape. The red blood cell has no nucleus, just a paler dip in the middle.

The sperm cell has a tail. The nerve cell is long with branches. The root hair cell has a long "hair". The egg cell is large with a nucleus and an outer coat. The red blood cell is a disc with a dip in the middle.

3.Match each cell to its job.

Drag each job onto a cell, or tap a job and then a cell.

Nerve cells carry messages. Root hair cells take in water. Red blood cells carry oxygen. Sperm cells swim to the egg, and the egg holds food for the embryo.

4.A root hair cell has a long, thin "hair" that pokes out into the soil. How does this help it do its job?

The long hair gives the cell a much larger surface area touching the soil, so it can take in more water (and minerals).

5.A red blood cell has no nucleus. How does this help it do its job?

Without a nucleus there is more space to carry oxygen. Its flat disc shape also gives a large surface for oxygen to get in and out quickly.

6.Some nerve cells are about a metre long, reaching from the bottom of your spine to your toes. Why is being long useful?

One long nerve cell carries a message all the way along it quickly. The branches at each end connect to other cells.

7.Tap all the features that help a sperm cell reach the egg. There are 2.

The tail lets the sperm swim, and its many mitochondria release the energy it needs to keep swimming.

8.An egg cell is many times bigger than a sperm cell. Why does it need to be so big?

The egg's large cytoplasm is a food store for the new embryo before it can get food any other way.

Challenge9.A scientist looks at an unknown cell under a microscope. It has a cell wall, a nucleus and a long thin part sticking out, but no chloroplasts. Which cell is it most likely to be?

Hint

Use one clue at a time. A cell wall tells you it is a plant or an animal cell. No chloroplasts tells you where in the plant it could be.

The cell wall means it is a plant cell. No chloroplasts means it gets no light, so it is underground. The long thin part is a root hair, so it is a root hair cell.
