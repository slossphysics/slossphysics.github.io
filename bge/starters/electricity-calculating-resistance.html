Calculating resistance

LI: I am learning to calculate resistance from voltage and current.

SC: I can use R = V / I · I can set out my working: relationship, substitution, answer with unit · I can spot mistakes in working.

5:00

1.Which relationship links resistance, voltage and current?

Resistance = voltage ÷ current: R = V / I.

2.Match each symbol to its quantity and unit.

Drag each symbol onto its quantity, or tap a symbol and then its quantity.

Hint

Start with the symbols that are the first letter of a quantity's name. Then see what is left.

R is resistance (Ω), V is voltage (V) and I is current (A).

3.The voltage across a resistor is 6 V and the current through it is 2 A. Calculate its resistance.

Ω

You've divided the current by the voltage. It's voltage divided by current.

You've multiplied. Resistance is voltage divided by current.

  • R = V / I
  • R = 6 / 2
  • R = 3 Ω

4.Spot the mistake. The voltage is 10 V and the current is 0.5 A. A pupil writes:

Hint

In R = V / I, V goes on top. Which number in the question is the voltage?

The voltage (10 V) goes on top: R = 10 / 0.5 = 20 Ω.

5.Use the meter readings to calculate the resistance of the resistor.

Ω

You've divided the current by the voltage. It's voltage divided by current.

You've multiplied. Resistance is voltage divided by current.

Hint

The meter with A gives the current. The meter with V gives the voltage.

  • R = V / I
  • R = 12 / 0.4
  • R = 30 Ω

6.What is the missing unit? R = 9 / 3 = 3 ___

R is resistance, so the unit is the ohm (Ω).

7.A desk lamp is plugged into the 230 V mains. The current through it is 0.5 A. Calculate the resistance of the lamp.

Ω

You've multiplied. Resistance is voltage divided by current.

You've divided the current by the voltage. It's voltage divided by current.

  • R = V / I
  • R = 230 / 0.5
  • R = 460 Ω

8.Each card gives the voltage across a resistor and the current through it. Sort the resistors by their resistance.

Drag each card into its group, or tap a card and then the group.

Using R = V / I: 6 V and 2 A is 3 Ω, 9 V and 3 A is 3 Ω, 5 V and 1 A is 5 Ω (all less than 10 Ω). 12 V and 0.5 A is 24 Ω, 20 V and 1 A is 20 Ω, 4 V and 0.1 A is 40 Ω (all more than 10 Ω).

Challenge9.A 20 Ω resistor has a current of 0.3 A through it. Calculate the voltage across it.

V

You've divided the resistance by the current. Rearrange R = V / I to get V on its own first.

You've divided the current by the resistance. Rearrange R = V / I to get V on its own first.

Hint

You know R and I. In R = V / I, V is being divided by I. What do you do to both sides to get V on its own?

  • V = IR
  • V = 0.3 × 20
  • V = 6 V
