Series or parallel?

1.Is this circuit series or parallel?

Hint

Put your finger on the cell and follow the wire all the way round. One path with no splits means series. If the wire splits at a junction (a dot), it is parallel.

There is only one path: the current goes through the switch and all three lamps, one after the other. That makes it a series circuit.

2.This one is drawn differently, with the cell in the middle. Is it series or parallel?

Hint

It doesn’t matter where the cell is drawn. Start at the cell and follow the wire to the first dot. Can the current go more than one way from there?

At the dot the current can go up to one lamp or down to the other. Each lamp is on its own branch, so this is a parallel circuit.

3.The lamps here are drawn on two rows. Is the circuit series or parallel?

Hint

Don’t go by the shape. Follow the wire from one end of the cell to the other. Do you pass through one lamp or both?

The wire never splits. The current goes through the top lamp and then the bottom lamp, so they are in series, even though they are drawn on two rows.

4.How many different paths can the current take from the cell, round the circuit and back to the cell?

Hint

Trace a loop from the cell, through one component, and back to the cell. Then see how many different loops like that you can find.

There are 3 branches: one through each lamp and one through the motor. Every path starts and ends at the cell.

7.The three lamps are identical. Which lamp is brightest?

Hint

How bright a lamp is depends on the current through it. Compare the current through X, through Y and through Z.

The current is the same all the way round a series circuit, and the lamps are identical, so they are all equally bright. No lamp gets the current “first”.

Current in parallel

8.The two lamps are identical. Which ammeter has the biggest reading?

Hint

For each ammeter, ask: does the current for the top lamp flow through it, the current for the bottom lamp, or both?

Q is next to the cell, so the current for both lamps flows through it. At the dots that current splits: P and R each carry half of it.

9.A lamp and a motor are in parallel. Ammeter X reads 0.20 A and ammeter Y reads 0.70 A. What does ammeter Z read?

A

That’s the difference between the branches. Z carries the current for the lamp and the motor.

Z isn’t the average of the two branches. The two branch currents join together at the dot before they reach Z.

That’s only the lamp’s branch. The motor’s current flows through Z as well.

That’s only the motor’s branch. The lamp’s current flows through Z as well.

The branch currents aren’t multiplied. They join together at the dot: what do you do with them?

Hint

Think of the dots as places where the current splits up and joins back together. All the current from both branches has to go through Z.

In parallel the branch currents add up to the current from the cell: 0.20 A + 0.70 A = 0.90 A.

10.The three lamps are all different. Ammeter Z reads 1.2 A, ammeter W reads 0.50 A and ammeter X reads 0.30 A. What does ammeter Y read?

A

That’s W and X added together. How much of Z’s 1.2 A is left for Y’s branch?

Changing the circuit

11.With one cell, the ammeter reads 0.20 A. A second cell is added, facing the same way. What happens to the ammeter reading?

Hint

The cell is what pushes the current round the loop. Think back to building circuits: what happened to a lamp when you added a cell?

Two cells give a bigger push (a bigger voltage) than one, so the current increases and the lamp is brighter.

12.Each card is a change to a circuit. Sort them: does the current from the cell get bigger or smaller?

Drag each card into its group, or tap a card and then the group.

Hint

Ask two things about each change. Is there more push or less push (cells)? Is it easier or harder for the current to get round: more in the same loop, or an extra path?

Bigger: more cells push harder, fewer lamps in a loop make it easier, and each new branch adds its own current. Smaller: fewer cells, more lamps in the same loop, or one branch fewer.

13.Lamps X and Y are in parallel. A third lamp is then added on a new branch. What happens to the reading on ammeter P, which is on lamp X’s branch?

Hint

Trace the loop from the cell, through P and lamp X, and back to the cell. Is the new lamp anywhere in that loop?

Lamp X still has its own loop to the cell, and nothing in that loop has changed, so P stays the same. The cell supplies extra current for the new branch, so the current from the cell goes up.
