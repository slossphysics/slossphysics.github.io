BGE · Second and Third level · Forces, electricity and waves · SCN 2-09a

Energy in circuits and safety

LI: I am learning how circuits transfer energy and how to use electricity safely.

SC: I can say what energy a component gives out · I can describe the energy changes in a circuit · I can spot electrical hazards.

5:00

1.Match each component to the useful energy it gives out.

Drag each component onto its energy, or tap a component and then its energy.

Each component transfers electrical energy into another form. The form it is designed to give out is its useful energy.

2.What does a battery store?

A battery is a store of chemical energy. In a circuit it transfers that energy to the components. It doesn’t store current.

3.A torch has a battery and a lamp. Which describes the energy changes when it is switched on?

Hint

Start with what the battery stores. Then think about what the wires carry, and what comes out of the lamp.

Chemical energy in the battery becomes electrical energy in the wires, which the lamp transfers into light (useful) and heat (wasted).

4.A filament lamp gets hot as well as giving out light. What is the heat?

We want light from a lamp, so the heat is wasted energy. It isn’t destroyed: it spreads out and warms the surroundings.

5.Tap all 3 appliances that are designed to make things hot.

A kettle, a toaster and an iron all have a heating element: heat is their useful energy.

6.Sort each of these into safe or hazard.

Drag each card into its group, or tap a card and then the group.

Water, damaged cables and overloaded sockets can all lead to electric shocks or fires.

7.Why do electrical cables have a plastic coating?

Plastic is an insulator: current can’t flow through it. The coating keeps the current inside the metal wire and away from you.

8.Why is it dangerous to touch a switch or plug with wet hands?

Tap water conducts electricity. Wet skin gives the current an easy path into your body, which can cause an electric shock.

9.Why is it dangerous to plug lots of appliances into one socket using adaptors?

Hint

Every appliance needs its own current, and all of it flows through the one socket. What happens to a wire when a big current flows through it?

Every appliance draws current through the same socket, so the total current gets big. A big current heats the wires, which can start a fire.

Challenge10.A hairdryer blows hot air to dry your hair. Sort the energy it gives out into useful and wasted.

Drag each card into its group, or tap a card and then the group.

Hint

For each card, ask: does this help to dry your hair? The same kind of energy can be useful in one place and wasted in another.

Heat can be useful or wasted: heat in the air dries your hair, but heat that warms the handle doesn’t help. The sound is wasted too.
