BGE · Fourth level · Forces, electricity and waves · SCN 4-09b

Input devices

LI: I am learning how input devices sense changes around them.

SC: I can name the symbols for input devices · I can describe how the resistance of an LDR and a thermistor changes · I can choose an input device for a job.

5:00

1.Match each symbol to its name.

Drag each symbol onto its name, or tap a symbol and then its name.

Hint

Start with the plain box: that is a resistor. Most of the others are the same box with something added: arrows, a circle, or a line through it. What could each extra mark stand for?

The LDR has arrows pointing in (light coming in). The thermistor has a line with a flat foot through it. The variable resistor has an arrow through it, because you can change it.

2.Sort these into input devices and output devices.

Drag each one into its group, or tap it and then the group.

Input devices sense a change (light, heat, sound, a press) and feed it into the circuit. Output devices do something we can see, hear or feel: give out light, sound or movement.

3.An LDR is a light-dependent resistor. What happens to its resistance as the light gets brighter?

In bright light an LDR's resistance is low. In the dark its resistance is high.

4.A thermistor is warmed up. What happens to its resistance?

As a thermistor gets warmer, its resistance goes down. When it is cold, its resistance is high.

5.Match each job to the best input device.

Drag each job onto a device, or tap a job and then a device.

Choose the device that senses the right thing: light (LDR), temperature (thermistor), sound (microphone), a press (switch), or a setting you turn or slide (variable resistor).

6.Tap all the devices whose resistance changes by itself when the conditions around them change. There are 2.

An LDR reacts to light and a thermistor reacts to temperature. That is why they make good sensors.

7.The resistance of the variable resistor in this circuit is turned up. What happens to the lamp?

More resistance means less current round the loop, so the lamp gets dimmer. This is how a dimmer switch works.

8.A pupil covers the LDR in this circuit with their hand. What happens to the ammeter reading?

Hint

Covering the LDR makes it dark. Is it easier or harder for current to get through an LDR in the dark?

In the dark the LDR's resistance goes up, so less current flows and the ammeter reading goes down.

Challenge9.A pupil connects a sensor to a 6.0 V supply and an ammeter. They change the temperature and record the current. The supply voltage stays the same. What do the results show?

Temperature (°C)Current (A)
200.10
400.15
600.24
800.40
Hint

The voltage is kept the same. If more current flows, is the sensor making it harder or easier for current to get through?

With the same voltage, a bigger current means a smaller resistance. The resistance falls as the temperature rises, which is how a thermistor behaves.
