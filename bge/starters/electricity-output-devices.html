BGE · Fourth level · Forces, electricity and waves · SCN 4-09b

Output devices

LI: I am learning how output devices work and how to use them safely in a circuit.

SC: I can say what different output devices give out · I can explain why an LED needs a resistor · I can describe what a relay does.

5:00

1.Sort these output devices by what they give out.

Drag each one into its group, or tap it and then the group.

Output devices change electrical energy into something useful: light (lamp, LED), sound (buzzer, loudspeaker) or movement (motor).

2.Match each symbol to its name.

Drag each symbol onto its name, or tap a symbol and then its name.

Hint

Look for small arrows pointing out of a symbol: they show light being given out. A letter in a circle tells you what the component is.

The LED symbol is a triangle with a bar, inside a circle, with arrows for the light coming out. The motor is a circle with M in it.

3.Match each job to the best output device.

Drag each job onto a device, or tap a job and then a device.

A buzzer makes one simple sound, but music needs a loudspeaker. An LED is small and uses little energy, so it suits an indicator light.

4.Why does an LED need a resistor in series with it?

Without a resistor, the current through an LED could be too big and damage it. The resistor limits the current.

5.Which circuit lights the LED safely?

The resistor must be in series with the LED (in the same loop), so the same, smaller current flows through both.

6.Spot the mistake: this LED doesn't light. The cell, the resistor and the wires all work. Why not?

Hint

An LED only lets current through one way. The triangle in its symbol points the way current can go. Current leaves the cell from the long line (+).

Current leaves the long line (+) of the cell and goes round the loop to the left, then along the top from left to right. The LED's triangle points the other way, so it blocks the current. Turn the LED round and it lights.

7.What is a relay?

When current flows through the relay's coil, it becomes an electromagnet and pulls a switch closed.

8.A car's starter motor needs a very big current. Why is it switched on using a relay?

Hint

A relay joins two circuits: one through its coil, and one through its switch. Which circuit does the ignition key control, and which one carries the motor's current?

The ignition switch only has to carry a small current through the relay's coil. The relay's switch then turns on the big current for the motor, which keeps the ignition switch and its thin wires safe.

Challenge9.LEDs X and Y are on separate parallel branches, each with its own resistor. Which LEDs light?

Hint

Current leaves the cell from the long line (+). Follow it to the dot where it splits, then along each branch. The triangle in an LED symbol points the way current can flow through it.

Current leaves the long line (+) of the cell, goes up the left side and, in both branches, flows from left to right. LED X points that way, so it lights. LED Y is the wrong way round, so it blocks the current in its own branch and stays off. X doesn't mind, because parallel branches work on their own.
