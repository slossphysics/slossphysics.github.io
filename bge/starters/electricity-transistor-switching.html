BGE · Fourth level · Forces, electricity and waves · SCN 4-09c

Transistor switching

LI: I am learning how a transistor can be used as an electronic switch.

SC: I can describe what a transistor does · I can sort a system into input, process and output · I can choose the input and output devices for an electronic system.

5:00

1.What job does a transistor do in an electronic circuit?

A transistor is an electronic switch. It switches an output on or off, with no moving parts and no one pressing it.

2.Match each symbol to its name.

Drag each symbol onto its name, or tap a symbol and then its name.

The transistor is the only one with three connections. Arrows pointing in mean the LDR senses light; arrows pointing out mean the LED gives out light.

3.An electronic system has an input, a process and an output. Sort these parts.

Drag each one into its group, or tap it and then the group.

Hint

The input senses a change. The output does something you can see, hear or feel. The process sits in between and decides whether to switch the output on.

Inputs sense (LDR, thermistor, microphone). The transistor processes: it switches. Outputs act (lamp, buzzer, motor).

4.What makes a transistor switch on?

When the voltage at the transistor's input (the base) is high enough, it switches on and current can flow through the output.

5.Why does a street light use a transistor, instead of a switch someone works by hand?

The LDR senses that it's getting dark and the transistor switches the lamp on automatically. Nobody has to go round every street light.

6.A gardener wants an alarm that sounds when it gets cold enough for frost. Which input and output devices are needed?

A thermistor senses temperature (input). A buzzer makes the warning sound (output). A transistor in between does the switching.

7.A greenhouse fan should switch on by itself when it gets too hot. Which system would work?

Input → process → output: a thermistor senses the heat, the transistor switches, and a motor turns the fan.

8.This is a street light circuit. Which labelled part is the input sensor that detects light?

Hint

Look for the symbol with arrows pointing in towards it. What might those arrows stand for?

Q is the LDR (arrows pointing in show light). It is the input. R is the transistor (process) and S is the lamp (output).

Challenge9.In this street light circuit, it is daytime and transistor R is switched off. Why is lamp S off, even though it is connected to the battery?

Hint

Start at the battery and follow the wire through lamp S. What else does the current have to pass through to get back to the battery?

Lamp S is in series with transistor R. When R is off it acts like an open switch: there is a gap in the lamp's loop, so no current flows. When it gets dark, R switches on, the loop is complete and the lamp lights.
