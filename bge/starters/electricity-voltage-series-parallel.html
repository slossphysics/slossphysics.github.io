Voltage in series and parallel

LI: I am learning how voltage behaves in series and parallel circuits.

SC: I can connect a voltmeter correctly · I can add up voltages in a series circuit · I can explain why each branch of a parallel circuit gets the supply voltage.

5:00

1.Match each word to what it means.

Drag each word onto its meaning, or tap a word and then its meaning.

A voltmeter measures voltage in volts (V). An ammeter measures current in amperes (A).

2.What does the voltage of a cell or battery tell you?

Voltage is the push of the supply: how much energy it gives to the charges. It is measured in volts (V).

3.Which circuit correctly measures the voltage across the lamp?

Hint

Check the letter in each meter first. Then look at whether the meter is in the loop, or joined on either side of the lamp.

A voltmeter (V) goes in parallel: across the lamp, with one wire joined on each side of it.

4.The two lamps are identical and the battery supplies 6.0 V. What does the voltmeter read?

V

In a series circuit the supply voltage is shared between the components.

The voltages across the lamps add up to the supply voltage, so each one must be less than 6.0 V.

Hint

In a series circuit, what must the voltages across the two lamps add up to?

The two identical lamps share the 6.0 V equally: 6.0 V ÷ 2 = 3.0 V.

5.The battery supplies 9.0 V. Voltmeter P reads 4.0 V across the lamp. What does voltmeter Q read across the motor?

V

The motor shares the battery’s 9.0 V with the lamp.

The lamp and the motor are different, so they don’t share the voltage equally. Use the battery voltage and P’s reading.

In series the voltage across each component isn’t always the same. What do they add up to?

The voltages across the components add up to the supply voltage, so Q must be less than 9.0 V.

In series the voltages add up to the supply: 4.0 V + Q = 9.0 V, so Q = 5.0 V.

6.The two lamps are in parallel and the battery supplies 4.5 V. What does the voltmeter across the top lamp read?

V

In a parallel circuit the voltage isn’t shared between the branches. Each branch is joined straight across the battery.

Voltages don’t add up across parallel branches. Each branch is joined straight across the battery.

Hint

Trace from one end of the battery, through the top lamp, and back to the other end. Is any other component in the way?

Each branch is joined directly across the battery, so each lamp gets the full 4.5 V.

7.Sort each statement into series or parallel.

Drag each card into its group, or tap a card and then the group.

Series: the components share the supply voltage. Parallel: every branch gets the full supply voltage.

8.All four lamps are identical and the two cells are the same. In which circuit are the lamps brighter?

In circuit B (parallel) each lamp gets the full voltage of the cell. In circuit A (series) the two lamps share it, so they are dimmer.

9.Three identical lamps are in series with a 6.0 V battery. What is the voltage across each lamp?

Three identical lamps share the 6.0 V equally: 6.0 V ÷ 3 = 2.0 V each. Check: 2.0 V + 2.0 V + 2.0 V = 6.0 V.

Challenge10.All three lamps are identical and the battery supplies 6.0 V. What does the voltmeter read?

V

The bottom branch does get the full 6.0 V, but two lamps in that branch share it.

The branches don’t share the voltage: each branch gets the full 6.0 V. Then think about the bottom branch on its own.

Hint

First work out the voltage across the whole bottom branch. Then think about how the two lamps in that branch share it.

Each branch gets the full 6.0 V. In the bottom branch, two identical lamps in series share it: 6.0 V ÷ 2 = 3.0 V.
