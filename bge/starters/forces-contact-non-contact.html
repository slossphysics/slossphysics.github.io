BGE · Second level · Forces, electricity and waves · SCN 2-08a

Contact and non-contact forces

LI: I am learning to compare magnetic, electrostatic and gravitational forces.

SC: I can sort forces into contact and non-contact · I can say which forces attract and which can also repel · I can explain how rubbing makes a charge · I can read a newton meter in newtons.

5:00

1.Sort these forces. Do the objects need to touch for the force to act?

Drag each card into its group, or tap a card and then the group.

Hint

Can a magnet pull a paper clip from a short distance away? Can you push a door without touching it?

Contact: friction, air resistance, water resistance and pushes need surfaces to touch. Non-contact: magnetic, electrostatic and gravitational forces act across a gap.

2.Two bar magnets are placed on a table with the north pole of one facing the south pole of the other. What happens?

Opposite poles (north and south) attract. Like poles (north and north, or south and south) repel.

3.Which forces can push things apart (repel) as well as pull them together (attract)? Tap all that apply.

Magnetic and electrostatic forces can attract or repel. Gravity only attracts.

4.A balloon is rubbed on a woolly jumper. Afterwards it sticks to the wall. Why?

Rubbing two surfaces together moves charge from one to the other, so the balloon becomes charged. The charge makes an electrostatic force that attracts the wall.

5.Two balloons are both rubbed on the same jumper, so they get the same kind of charge. They are hung next to each other. What happens?

Hint

Do the two balloons have the same charge or opposite charges?

Both balloons have the same kind of charge, and like charges repel, so they swing apart.

6.A bag hangs from this newton meter. What is the reading?

N

A newton meter's scale starts at 0 at the top. Count down from 0, not up from the bottom.

The numbers go up in 2s, but each small mark is only 1 N.

Look again: the pointer is one mark below the 6.

Hint

The numbers go up in 2s. How many newtons is each small mark worth?

Each small mark is 1 N. The pointer is one mark below 6, so the reading is 7 N. This is the force of gravity on the bag.

7.Match each force to something it is used for.

Drag each force onto its use, or tap a force and then its use.

A compass needle is a small magnet. A rubbed comb is charged, so it uses an electrostatic force. Water flows downhill because of gravity.

Challenge8.You have two metal rods that look the same. One is a magnet. The other is plain iron, which is not a magnet. You hold each end of each rod near the north pole of a bar magnet. What would prove that a rod is a magnet?

Hint

Think about what a plain piece of iron can never do near a magnet.

Both rods are attracted to a magnet, so attraction proves nothing. Only a magnet can repel: if one end pushes the north pole away, that end is a north pole, so the rod must be a magnet.
