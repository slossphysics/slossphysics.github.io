Floating, sinking and density

LI: I am learning to explain floating and sinking using density.

SC: I can use density, not weight, to decide whether something floats in water · I can calculate density using ρ = m / V · I can explain why a steel ship floats.

5:00

1.A huge tree trunk floats on a loch, but a small steel coin sinks. Why?

Hint

Is it how heavy something is, or how heavy it is for its size, that matters?

Floating depends on density, not weight. Wood is less dense than water, so it floats however big it is. Steel is more dense than water, so a steel coin sinks however small it is.

2.Each card gives a material and its density. Sort the materials: do solid blocks of them float or sink in water?

Drag each card into its group, or tap a card and then the group.

Hint

The density of water is 1 g/cm³. Compare each material with that.

Water has a density of 1 g/cm³. Anything less dense floats: cork, pine wood and ice. Anything more dense sinks: brick, glass, aluminium and iron.

3.Which relationship is used to calculate density?

Density = mass ÷ volume: ρ = m / V. With mass in g and volume in cm³, density is in g/cm³. With mass in kg and volume in m³, density is in kg/m³.

4.A small block of stone has a mass of 60 g and a volume of 20 cm³. Calculate its density.

g/cm³

You've divided the volume by the mass. Density is mass divided by volume.

You've multiplied. Density is mass divided by volume.

You've added. Density is mass divided by volume.

  • ρ = m / V
  • ρ = 60 / 20
  • ρ = 3 g/cm³

This is more than 1 g/cm³, so the stone sinks in water.

6.True or false? A pupil says: "A big steel bolt sinks. But if you cut off a tiny piece of the steel, it will float, because it is so light."

Hint

When you cut the steel, both its mass and its volume get smaller. What happens to mass ÷ volume?

False. Cutting the steel makes both the mass and the volume smaller, so the density stays the same, about 7.9 g/cm³. That is more than water's 1 g/cm³, so the tiny piece still sinks.

7.Steel is about 8 times as dense as water, yet a huge steel ship floats. Why?

Hint

Think about the whole ship, including everything inside the hull. Is the inside solid steel?

The steel is shaped into a thin, hollow hull with lots of air inside. The total mass of the ship divided by its total volume is less than the density of water, so it floats. Shape matters: the same steel squashed into a solid lump would sink.

8.A concrete block has a volume of 2 m³ and a mass of 4800 kg. Calculate the density of concrete.

kg/m³

You've divided the volume by the mass. Density is mass divided by volume.

You've multiplied. Density is mass divided by volume.

  • ρ = m / V
  • ρ = 4800 / 2
  • ρ = 2400 kg/m³

Water is 1000 kg/m³, so concrete sinks.

Challenge9.A solid plastic toy has a mass of 0.12 kg and a volume of 150 cm³. Calculate its density in g/cm³. Will it float or sink in water?

g/cm³

You've used the mass in kilograms. The answer is wanted in g/cm³, so change the mass into grams first.

You've divided the volume by the mass. Density is mass divided by volume.

You've divided the volume by the mass, and the mass is still in kilograms. Change kg to g, then do mass ÷ volume.

Hint

The volume is in cm³, so the mass needs to be in grams. There are 1000 g in 1 kg.

Mass = 0.12 kg = 120 g.

  • ρ = m / V
  • ρ = 120 / 150
  • ρ = 0.8 g/cm³

This is less than water's 1 g/cm³, so the toy floats.
