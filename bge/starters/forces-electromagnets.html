BGE · Fourth level · Forces, electricity and waves · SCN 4-08a

Electromagnets

LI: I am learning what affects the strength of an electromagnet and how electromagnets and supermagnets are used.

SC: I can name three ways to make an electromagnet stronger · I can draw a conclusion from a table of results · I can describe some uses of electromagnets and supermagnets.

5:00

1.What is an electromagnet?

An electromagnet is a coil of wire that becomes magnetic when a current flows through it. It is usually wrapped round an iron core to make it stronger.

2.The diagram shows a simple electromagnet made in class. What is the part marked X?

X is the iron core: the bar the coil is wrapped around, often an iron nail in class. It makes the electromagnet much stronger, and it stops being magnetic when the current is switched off.

3.Which of these changes would make an electromagnet stronger? Tap all that apply.

An electromagnet is stronger with more turns on the coil, a bigger current, and an iron core.

How many more paperclips does it pick up each time 10 turns are added?

Each extra 10 turns picks up 4 more paperclips, so 50 turns gives 16 + 4 = 20 paperclips.

6.A pupil wants to find out whether a bigger current makes an electromagnet stronger. To make it a fair test, what must she keep the same?

She changes only the current and measures the paperclips. Everything else that affects strength, the number of turns and the core, must stay the same.

7.A scrapyard crane uses a large electromagnet instead of a permanent magnet to lift old cars. Why?

An electromagnet can be switched on and off. The crane switches it on to lift a car and switches it off to drop it. A permanent magnet would never let go.

8.Strong electromagnets and supermagnets are used in lots of devices. Match each device to how it uses a magnet.

Drag each device onto its description, or tap a device and then its description.

MRI scanners use very strong magnets to image the body. Hard drives store data magnetically. Electric guitar pickups turn string vibrations into a signal. Loudspeakers use a magnet to vibrate a cone. Electric and hybrid car motors use magnets and a current to turn the wheels.
