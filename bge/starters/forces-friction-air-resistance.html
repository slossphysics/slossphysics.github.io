BGE · Second level · Forces, electricity and waves · SCN 2-07a

Friction and air resistance

LI: I am learning how friction and air resistance affect moving objects.

SC: I can describe friction as a force that opposes motion · I can say how speed and surface area change air resistance · I can give examples of friction being useful and being a problem.

5:00

1.What is friction?

Friction is a force that opposes motion. It acts when two surfaces rub together, or when something moves through air or water.

2.A box is sliding to the right along the floor. Which arrow shows the friction on the box?

Hint

Friction acts where the two surfaces rub, and it always works against the way the object is moving.

Friction acts along the floor, opposite to the motion. The box moves right, so friction points left: arrow C.

3.Which of these involve friction or a resistance force acting against the motion? Tap all that apply.

The sledge rubs on the snow (friction), the swimmer pushes through water (water resistance) and the skydiver pushes through air (air resistance). The magnet's pull is a magnetic force, not friction.

4.Is friction useful or a problem here? Sort each example.

Drag each card into its group, or tap a card and then the group.

Friction is useful when we want grip or want to slow down: brakes, shoe grip, parachutes. It is a problem when it wears moving parts away or slows down something we want to go fast: engine parts, bike chains, a racing cyclist.

5.Two identical sheets of paper are dropped from the same height at the same time. One is crumpled into a ball and one is left flat. Which falls more slowly?

Hint

Both sheets weigh the same. Which one has more surface pushing against the air as it falls?

The flat sheet (B) has a bigger surface area pushing against the air, so it has more air resistance and falls more slowly.

6.A cyclist pedals harder and goes faster. What happens to the air resistance on the cyclist?

The faster something moves through the air, the bigger the air resistance on it.

7.True or false? "A ball rolling across the grass stops because it runs out of force." Choose the answer with the right reason.

False. The ball doesn't carry a force that runs out. Friction from the grass and air resistance act against its motion and slow it down. On a smooth, flat floor there is less friction, so it rolls much further.

8.Match each design to what it does.

Drag each design onto what it does, or tap a design and then what it does.

Tyre tread gives grip, brake pads rub to slow the wheel, a parachute has a big surface area to increase air resistance, and a streamlined racing car lets air flow past it easily.

Challenge9.A skydiver jumps from a plane. At first she falls faster and faster. After a while she stops speeding up, even before she opens her parachute. Why?

Hint

Two forces act on her: gravity pulling down and air resistance pushing up. Which one changes as she gets faster?

Air resistance increases with speed. As she falls faster, the air pushes up harder, until it balances the pull of gravity. Then she stops speeding up and falls at a steady speed.
