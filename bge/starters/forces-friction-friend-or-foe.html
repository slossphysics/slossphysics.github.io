Friction: friend or foe?

LI: I am recapping when friction helps us, when it gets in the way, and how to reduce it.

SC: I can sort examples of friction into helpful and unhelpful · I can choose a way to reduce friction · I can use results to find the best lubricant.

5:00

1.Fill in the blanks about reducing friction. Two of the words are not needed.

Drag each word into its gap, or tap a word and then the gap.

Friction turns movement energy into wasted heat. Lubrication (oil or grease) makes surfaces slippery, and a streamlined shape lets air or water flow smoothly past.

2.Friend or foe? Sort each example: is the friction helping, or is it a nuisance?

Drag each card into its group, or tap a card and then the group.

Hint

Ask yourself: do you want things to grip or rub here, or would it be better if they slid easily?

Friend: gloves grip the ball, a match needs rubbing to light, and a pencil only writes because it rubs on the paper. Foe: a sticking drawer and a rusty wheel are harder to move, and rubbing wears your sock away.

3.The slide at the park is far too slow. Which of these would get you down it fastest?

A smooth plastic mat has fewer bumps to catch on the slide, so there is less friction and you go faster. Rubber soles, sand and gripping the sides all add friction.

4.Each of these has a friction problem. Match each problem to the best fix.

Drag each problem onto its fix, or tap a problem and then the fix.

Wheels roll instead of sliding, oil lubricates the hinge, a streamlined shape lets air flow past the cyclist, and smooth, waxed runners have fewer bumps to catch on the snow.

5.Dolphins, sharks and penguins are very different animals, but they all have the same kind of body shape: rounded at the front, smooth, and narrow at the tail. Why?

They are all streamlined. Water flows smoothly around them, so there is less water resistance and they waste less energy when they swim. Fast boats and submarines copy the same shape.

6.Sometimes we add friction on purpose. Which of these are done to increase friction? Tap all that apply.

Chalk stops a gymnast's hands slipping on the bars, and studs dig into the grass so a footballer can turn without sliding. Oil and polish both reduce friction.

7.A class pulls the same wooden block across a table with a newton balance. They try it dry, then with a different liquid spread on the table each time. Which liquid was the best lubricant?

Hint

The newton balance shows how hard the class had to pull. If there is less friction, is the block easier or harder to pull?

With washing-up liquid the block needed the smallest pull (1 N), so there was the least friction. The best lubricant is the one that reduces friction the most.

8.In curling, players sweep the ice hard with brushes just in front of the sliding stone. Why?

Sweeping warms and smooths the top of the ice, so there is less friction. The stone loses its movement energy more slowly and slides further.
