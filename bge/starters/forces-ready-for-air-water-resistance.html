Recap: friction and measuring forces

1.Fill in the blanks about friction. Two of the words are not needed.

Drag each word into its gap, or tap a word and then the gap.

Friction is a that acts when two surfaces against each other.

Friction always acts in the direction to the way the object is moving.

Friction, like every force, is measured in .

Friction is a force that acts when two surfaces rub together. It acts in the opposite direction to the motion, so it slows things down. All forces are measured in newtons (N).

2.A block is pulled to the right across a table with a newton balance. Which arrow shows the friction on the block?

Hint

Friction acts where the block rubs on the table, and it always works against the way the block is moving.

Friction acts along the table, opposite to the motion. The block is pulled to the right, so friction points left: arrow B.

Hint

The numbers go up in 2s. How many newtons is each small mark worth?

Each small mark is 1 N. The pointer is one mark below 2, so the reading is 3 N. The newton balance measures the pull of gravity on the pencil case: its weight.

4.A class pulls the same shoe across four surfaces with a newton balance, at a slow, steady speed. Which surface has the most friction?

SurfaceNewton balance reading (N)
Wooden floor2
Sandpaper6
Carpet4
Rubber mat9
Hint

The more friction there is, the harder you have to pull. What does a harder pull do to the newton balance reading?

The bigger the friction, the harder you have to pull to keep the shoe moving, so the bigger the reading. The rubber mat needs the biggest pull (9 N), so it has the most friction, even though sandpaper feels rougher.

5.Is the friction useful or a problem here? Sort each example.

Drag each card into its group, or tap a card and then the group.

Friction is useful when we need grip or heat: tyres on grit, boot studs, striking a match. It is a problem when it wears things away, makes them stick, or wastes energy as heat: worn trainers, squeaky hinges, hot engine parts.

Looking ahead: air and water resistance

6.Friction doesn’t only happen between solid surfaces. Things moving through air or water are slowed down too. Sort each example by the force slowing it down.

Drag each card into its group, or tap a card and then the group.

Hint

Ask: what is this object pushing through or rubbing against? Air, water, or a solid surface?

Air resistance slows things moving through air, and water resistance slows things moving through water. Both are kinds of friction: they act against the motion, just like friction between solid surfaces.

7.A skydiver opens their parachute and slows down. Why does the parachute slow them down?

Hint

Think about holding a big sheet of card and running with it. What do you feel?

A parachute has a large surface area, so it pushes against lots of air. That makes the air resistance much bigger, and the skydiver slows down. Gravity still pulls them down, just as hard as before.

8.A speedboat goes faster and faster across a loch. What happens to the water resistance on the boat?

The faster something moves through water (or air), the bigger the resistance on it. That’s why it is so hard to run through a swimming pool, and why a boat needs more and more force to go faster.

Challenge9.A pupil wants to find out which of three shoes has the best grip. They will pull each shoe with a newton balance. Which things must they do to make it a fair test? Tap all that apply.

Hint

Only the shoe should change. A heavier shoe presses down harder on the floor: would that change the friction?

In a fair test only one thing changes: here, the shoe. Keep the surface the same, give every shoe the same mass (a heavier shoe presses down harder, which gives more friction), and pull slowly at a steady speed so the reading is steady.
