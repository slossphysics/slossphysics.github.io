BGE · Third level · Forces, electricity and waves · SCN 3-07a

Reducing friction

LI: I am learning how reducing friction makes moving systems more efficient.

SC: I can explain that friction turns movement energy into heat · I can describe how lubrication, streamlining and other methods reduce friction · I can use results to compare friction on different surfaces.

5:00

1.Rub your hands together quickly for a few seconds. What happens, and why?

Friction turns movement (kinetic) energy into heat. That's why your hands get warm. Energy is never destroyed, but heat from friction is usually wasted.

2.Match each method to how it reduces friction.

Drag each method onto how it works, or tap a method and then how it works.

Hint

Lubrication means adding oil or grease. Streamlining is about the shape of something moving through air or water.

Lubrication (oil) keeps the surfaces apart, streamlining lets air flow smoothly past, ball bearings roll instead of sliding, and smooth surfaces have fewer bumps to catch.

3.Each shape moves to the right through the air. Which shape is the most streamlined?

C is streamlined: its rounded front parts the air smoothly and its tapered back lets the air close up again behind it. Less air resistance means less energy wasted.

4.Does each of these reduce friction or increase it? Sort them.

Drag each card into its group, or tap a card and then the group.

Reduce: oil (lubrication), wax (a smooth, slippery surface), a streamlined helmet. Increase: grit and deep tread give grip, and brake pads rub to slow the wheel.

5.Why do car makers try to reduce friction between the moving parts in an engine?

Friction turns some of the engine's energy into wasted heat. Reducing it means more energy does the useful job of moving the car, so the engine is more efficient and uses less fuel.

6.True or false? "A well-oiled machine is more efficient than a dry one." Choose the answer with the right reason.

True. Oil (lubrication) reduces friction between the moving parts, so less energy is turned into wasted heat. More of the energy put in does the useful job: the machine is more efficient.

7.A class rolls the same toy car down the same ramp onto different surfaces. They measure how far it travels before it stops. Which surface has the least friction?

SurfaceDistance travelled (m)
Carpet0.6
Wood1.8
Sandpaper0.3
Plastic sheet2.4
Hint

Friction slows the car down. If there is less friction, does the car travel further or a shorter distance?

The car went furthest on the plastic sheet (2.4 m), so that surface slowed it down least: it has the least friction.

8.Spot the mistake. A pupil writes: "Friction always wastes energy, so the best bike would have no friction at all." What is wrong with this?

Some friction is useful: brakes need it to stop the bike and tyres need it to grip the road. Engineers reduce friction where it wastes energy (the chain, the wheel hubs) and keep it where it is useful.

J

You've added the two amounts of wasted heat. Work out the useful energy before and after, then compare them.

Hint

Useful energy = energy put in − energy wasted. Work it out before oiling and after oiling.

  • Useful before = 200 − 60 = 140 J
  • Useful after = 200 − 20 = 180 J
  • Extra useful energy = 180 − 140 = 40 J
