Speed–time graphs

LI: I am learning to describe and analyse motion using speed–time graphs.

SC: I can describe motion from the shape of a speed–time graph · I can calculate acceleration from a graph · I can find the distance travelled from the area under a graph.

5:00

1.The speed–time graph shows the motion of a car. What is the car doing?

Hint

On a speed–time graph, the height of the line tells you the speed. Does the height change?

A flat (horizontal) line means the speed stays the same. The line is at 12 m/s, so the car moves at a constant speed of 12 m/s. A flat line on a speed–time graph does not mean stopped.

2.Each small graph is a speed–time graph, with speed up the side and time along the bottom. Match each graph to the motion it shows.

Drag each graph onto its description, or tap a graph and then its description.

Hint

Follow each line from left to right. Is the speed getting bigger, getting smaller, staying the same, or zero the whole time?

Line sloping up: speeding up. Line sloping down: slowing down. Flat line above the axis: constant speed. Flat line along the time axis: speed is zero, so stationary.

3.The graph shows a bus travelling between two stops. In which section is the bus slowing down?

In section C the line slopes down from 12 m/s to 0 m/s, so the bus is slowing down (until it stops at the next stop). In A it speeds up and in B it moves at a constant speed.

4.True or false? A pupil looks at this speed–time graph and says: "The line goes down, so the cyclist is going backwards."

Hint

The height of the line is the speed. What happens to the speed between 0 s and 8 s?

False. The speed falls from 16 m/s to 4 m/s, so the cyclist is slowing down but still moving forwards the whole time. A speed–time graph shows how fast, not which way.

5.The graph shows a car pulling away from traffic lights. Calculate the car's acceleration during the first 8 s.

m/s²

You've divided the time by the change in speed. Acceleration is change in speed ÷ time.

You've multiplied. Acceleration is change in speed divided by time.

The car only speeds up for the first 8 s. After that the line is flat, so use t = 8 s.

That's the final speed. Acceleration is how much the speed changes each second.

Hint

Read the speed at the start and at 8 s. Acceleration = change in speed ÷ time taken.

  • a = (v − u) / t
  • a = (16 − 0) / 8
  • a = 2 m/s²

6.Spot the mistake. A pupil uses this graph to calculate a cyclist's acceleration during the first 6 s.

Hint

Where does the line start on the speed axis?

The cyclist was already moving at 4 m/s. The change in speed is 16 − 4 = 12 m/s, so a = (16 − 4) / 6 = 2 m/s².

7.The graph shows a runner during a race. Calculate the distance the runner travels in the 10 s shown.

m

You've divided time by speed. Distance is the area under the graph: speed × time for a rectangle.

You've added. The area of a rectangle is length × height.

Hint

The distance travelled is the area under the line on a speed–time graph. What shape is that area here?

  • distance = area under graph
  • distance = 8 × 10
  • distance = 80 m

The area under this line is a triangle. Area of a triangle = ½ × base × height.
