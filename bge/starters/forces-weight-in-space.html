BGE · Third level · Forces, electricity and waves · SCN 3-08a

Weight on other planets and in space

LI: I am learning to predict what happens to weight in different places on Earth and in space.

SC: I can explain that mass stays the same but weight changes with gravitational field strength · I can use W = mg with different values of g · I can explain why there is still gravity in space.

5:00

1.An astronaut has a mass of 70 kg on Earth. She travels to the Moon. What happens to her mass?

Mass is the amount of matter in an object, so it stays the same wherever she goes: still 70 kg on the Moon.

2.An astronaut travels from Earth (g = 9.8 N/kg) to the Moon (g = 1.6 N/kg). What happens to her weight?

The Moon's gravitational field strength is smaller, so her weight decreases. On the Moon she weighs about one sixth of her Earth weight.

Drag each card into its group, or tap a card and then the group.

Hint

Compare each value of g with Earth's 9.8 N/kg. A bigger g means a bigger pull on the same mass.

Where g is bigger than 9.8 N/kg (Jupiter, Saturn, Neptune) an object weighs more. Where g is smaller (Moon, Venus, Mars, Uranus) it weighs less. Its mass is the same everywhere.

4.An astronaut with a mass of 60 kg lands on Mars, where g = 3.7 N/kg. Calculate her weight on Mars.

N

That's her weight on Earth. Use the value of g for Mars.

You've divided the mass by g. Weight is mass multiplied by g.

You've added. Weight is mass multiplied by g.

  • W = mg
  • W = 60 × 3.7
  • W = 222 N

5.A climber stands on the top of Mount Everest, nearly 9 km above sea level. Compared with her weight at sea level, her weight is:

Hint

Is the climber closer to or further from the centre of the Earth than at sea level?

Gravitational field strength gets weaker the further you are from the centre of the Earth, so her weight is slightly less. Her mass doesn't change.

6.True or false? "There is no gravity on the International Space Station (ISS). That's why the astronauts float." Choose the answer with the right reason.

False. The ISS is only about 400 km up, and the Earth's gravity still pulls strongly on it and on the astronauts. They float because the ISS and everything in it are falling around the Earth together.

7.A spacecraft is in deep space, very far from any star or planet. What can you say about the astronaut inside?

Far from any star or planet, g is almost zero, so her weight is almost zero. Her mass is unchanged because she is made of the same amount of matter.

8.Spot the mistake. A pupil works out the weight of a 20 kg robot dog on the Moon, where g = 1.6 N/kg:

On the Moon, g = 1.6 N/kg: W = 20 × 1.6 = 32 N.

Challenge9.A space probe has a weight of 2480 N on Jupiter, where g = 24.8 N/kg. Calculate its weight on Earth, where g = 9.8 N/kg.

N

That's the probe's mass in kg. Now use W = mg with Earth's value of g.

You've divided the Jupiter weight by Earth's g. First find the mass using Jupiter's g.

You've multiplied the Jupiter weight by Earth's g. First find the mass using Jupiter's g.

Hint

The probe's mass is the same on Jupiter and on Earth. Find the mass first, using the Jupiter values.

  • On Jupiter: W = mg
  • 2480 = m × 24.8
  • m = 100 kg
  • On Earth: W = mg
  • W = 100 × 9.8
  • W = 980 N
