BGE · Second and Third level · Biological systems · SCN 2-14b, SCN 3-14b

Inherited characteristics, DNA and genes

LI: I am learning which characteristics are passed on from parents and how DNA carries them.

SC: I can sort characteristics into inherited and not inherited · I can describe where DNA is found and what it does · I can explain what a gene is.

5:00

1.Sort these characteristics into inherited and not inherited.

Drag each one into its group, or tap it and then the group.

Hint

Ask: were you born with it, from your parents' genes? Or did it come from something you learned, chose or had happen to you?

Inherited characteristics come from your parents' genes. Things you learn, choose or that happen to you during your life are not inherited.

2.A man trains at the gym for years and builds big muscles. Will his children be born with big muscles because of his training?

Muscles built by training are acquired during his life. They don't change his genes, so they are not inherited. The same goes for scars, tans and dyed hair.

3.Where is DNA found in most cells?

DNA is kept in the nucleus, the control centre of the cell.

4.What does DNA do?

DNA carries the genetic code: the instructions for how a living thing develops and how its body works.

5.Tap all the things that contain DNA. There are 4.

Every living thing has DNA: plants, fungi, bacteria and animals, not just humans. Things that have never been alive have none.

6.What is a gene?

A gene is a section of DNA that controls one characteristic, such as eye colour.

7.Match each word to what it means.

Drag each meaning onto its word, or tap a meaning and then its word.

Hint

These fit inside each other like boxes: a cell has a nucleus, the nucleus holds DNA, and DNA is made up of genes.

From biggest to smallest: a cell contains a nucleus, which holds DNA, which is divided into genes.

8.Two sisters have the same parents, but they don't look exactly alike. They are not twins. Why?

Each child gets half their genes from each parent, but a different combination each time. That is why every person (except identical twins) has a unique set of genes.

Challenge9.Identical twins have exactly the same genes. Two identical twins are brought up in different countries. As adults, one speaks Gaelic and has a suntan; the other speaks Polish and has a scar on her arm. Which of these differences are caused by their genes?

Hint

If two people have exactly the same genes, can a difference between them come from their genes?

Their genes are identical, so every difference between them must come from their environment and their lives: what they learned, where they lived and what happened to them.
