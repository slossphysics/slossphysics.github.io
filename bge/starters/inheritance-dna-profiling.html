BGE · Third level · Biological systems · SCN 3-14b

DNA profiling

LI: I am learning how DNA profiles can be used to identify people.

SC: I can compare DNA profiles to find a match · I can use DNA profiles in a paternity test · I can give arguments for and against keeping DNA records.

5:00

1.What is a DNA profile?

A DNA profile is a pattern of bands made by analysing a sample of someone's DNA. Everyone's pattern is unique, except identical twins.

2.Blood was found at a crime scene. Which suspect's DNA profile matches it?

Hint

Run your finger across from each crime scene band. For a match, every band must line up exactly, with none missing and none extra.

Every band in Suspect 2's profile lines up with a band from the crime scene, and there are no extra bands. The other suspects each have two bands in different places.

3.Why can a DNA profile be used to identify one person?

Every individual has a unique combination of genes, so their DNA profile is unique too. Identical twins are the one exception.

4.Tap all the things a forensic scientist could get DNA from at a crime scene. There are 4.

DNA can be taken from anything that contains the person's cells: blood, saliva, hair roots and skin.

5.In a paternity test, every band in a child's DNA profile must match a band from…

A child gets half their DNA from each parent. So each band comes from the mother or the father.

6.A paternity test compares a child's DNA profile with the mother's and two men's. Which man could be the child's father?

Hint

First find the child's bands that match the mother. The bands left over must have come from the father.

Two of the child's bands match the mother. The other two must come from the father, and Man 1 has both of them. Man 2 is missing one.

7.Some people think everyone's DNA profile should be kept on a national database. Sort these points into arguments for and against.

Drag each one into its group, or tap it and then the group.

A database could help solve crimes and clear innocent people. But it raises worries about privacy, security and who owns and uses the information.

8.Tap all the real uses of DNA profiling. There are 3.

DNA profiling is used to identify people: in forensics, in paternity tests, and to identify people after disasters.

Challenge9.Blood at a crime scene came from two people: the victim and one other person. Whose blood is the other?

Hint

Cross out the crime scene bands that belong to the victim. Which suspect matches exactly the bands that are left?

Take away the victim's three bands and three are left. Suspect B has exactly those three. Suspect C shares bands with the victim but is missing two of the leftover bands.
