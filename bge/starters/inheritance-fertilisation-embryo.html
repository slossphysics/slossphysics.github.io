BGE · Third level · Biological systems · SCN 3-14a

Fertilisation and the embryo

LI: I am learning how a baby starts to develop and what can put it at risk.

SC: I can describe fertilisation · I can describe what the placenta, umbilical cord and amniotic fluid do · I can give examples of harmful substances that can cross the placenta.

5:00

1.Egg cells and sperm cells are sex cells. What is the scientific name for sex cells?

Sex cells are called gametes. The female gamete is the egg and the male gamete is the sperm.

2.How much of the genetic information needed to make a new person does one sex cell contain?

Each gamete carries half the genetic information. When an egg and a sperm join, the two halves make a complete set.

3.Which of these is the moment of fertilisation?

Fertilisation is when the nucleus of the sperm fuses with the nucleus of the egg, bringing the two halves of the genetic information together.

4.What happens to a fertilised egg next?

The fertilised egg divides into 2 cells, then 4, then 8, and so on. This ball of cells is the embryo.

5.The diagram shows a baby growing in the womb. Match each part to its letter.

Drag each name onto its letter, or tap a name and then its letter.

Hint

One part is attached to the wall of the womb. One part joins the baby to it. One part is the liquid all around the baby.

A is the placenta, attached to the wall of the womb. B is the umbilical cord, joining the baby to the placenta. C is the amniotic fluid surrounding the baby.

6.Match each part to its job.

Drag each job onto its part, or tap a job and then its part.

The placenta is where substances pass between the mother's blood and the baby's. The umbilical cord links the baby to it. The amniotic fluid protects the baby inside the womb.

7.Tap all the substances that can cross the placenta and harm the baby. There are 3.

Alcohol, nicotine and carbon monoxide can all pass from the mother's blood into the baby's. So can some drugs and medicines, which is why pregnant women check with a doctor before taking anything.

8.Babies of mothers who smoke during pregnancy are more likely to be born small. Which is the best explanation?

Hint

The baby gets everything it needs through the placenta. What else could travel the same way?

Chemicals such as carbon monoxide and nicotine cross the placenta. Carbon monoxide means the baby's blood carries less oxygen, so the baby may not grow as well.

Challenge9.A baby has some characteristics from its mother and some from its father. Which is the best explanation?

Hint

Think about the very first moment the baby's genetic information is complete. What joined at that moment?

Each gamete has half the genetic information. At fertilisation the nuclei fuse, so the embryo gets one half from the mother and one half from the father. Everything after that, including the placenta, only feeds and protects it.
