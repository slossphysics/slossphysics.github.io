BGE · Fourth level · Biological systems · SCN 4-14c

Genetic crosses

LI: I am learning how characteristics are passed from parents to their offspring.

SC: I can use the words allele, dominant and recessive · I can complete a Punnett square · I can work out the chance of offspring having a characteristic.

5:00

1.Match each word to what it means.

Drag each meaning onto its word, or tap a meaning and then its word.

A gene can come in different versions called alleles. A dominant allele (capital letter) shows whenever it is there. A recessive allele (small letter) only shows if both alleles are recessive.

2.In pea plants, the allele for tall (T) is dominant and the allele for short (t) is recessive. A pea plant has the alleles Tt. What does it look like?

The plant has one dominant tall allele, so it is tall. The short allele is still there, and can be passed on, but it doesn't show.

3.In pea plants, tall (T) is dominant over short (t). Tap all the pairs of alleles that give a tall plant. There are 2.

Any plant with at least one T is tall: TT or Tt. Only tt is short.

Every offspring gets a T from one parent and a t from the other, so they are all Tt, and all tall.

5.Having an extra finger on each hand is caused by a dominant allele. Does this mean most people have an extra finger on each hand?

Dominant does not mean common. It means that if someone has the allele, it shows. Very few people have the allele for an extra finger, so the characteristic is rare.
