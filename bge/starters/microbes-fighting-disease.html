BGE · Third level · Biological systems · SCN 3-13c

Fighting disease

LI: I am learning how the body defends itself against disease and how vaccines protect us.

SC: I can describe the body's barriers to microbes · I can explain what white blood cells and antibodies do · I can explain how vaccination protects people and populations.

5:00

1.What causes infectious diseases such as flu and food poisoning?

Infectious diseases are caused by microbes, mainly bacteria and viruses (and some fungi), that get into the body.

2.Your body has barriers that stop microbes getting in: its first line of defence. Match each barrier to how it protects you.

Drag each description onto a barrier, or tap a description and then a barrier.

Skin keeps microbes out, mucus traps them, stomach acid kills them, and a blood clot seals a cut. These barriers are the first line of defence.

3.A deep cut that isn't cleaned can become infected. Why?

Unbroken skin is a barrier. A cut makes a way in for microbes, which is why we clean cuts and cover them.

4.If microbes do get into the body, white blood cells fight them. Tap all the things white blood cells do. There are 2.

Hint

Antibiotics and antibodies sound alike but are very different. Which one does your own body make?

Some white blood cells engulf (surround and digest) microbes. Others make antibodies, which stick to microbes and help destroy them.

5.Which statement about antibodies is true?

An antibody fits one particular microbe, a bit like a key fits one lock. A new microbe needs a new type of antibody.

6.Most people only catch chickenpox once. Why don't they catch it again?

After an infection the body remembers the microbe. Next time, the right antibodies are made so fast that the microbes are destroyed before you feel ill. You are immune.

7.What is in a vaccine?

A vaccine contains dead or weakened microbes, or parts of them. They can't make you ill, but your white blood cells still learn to make the right antibodies.

8.Put the steps of how a vaccine protects you in order.

Drag each step onto its place, or tap a step and then its place.

The vaccine acts like a practice run. The body makes antibodies and remembers how, so it is ready when the real microbe arrives.

9.A baby is too young to have the measles vaccine. Nearly everyone else at their nursery and at home has been vaccinated. How does this protect the baby?

Hint

For a disease to spread, it has to pass from one person who has it to the next. What happens if most people can't catch it?

When most people are vaccinated, the microbe has nobody to spread through. This also protects people who can't have the vaccine, such as young babies. It is sometimes called herd immunity.

Challenge10.People can catch chickenpox only once, but they can catch flu again and again. Many people get a new flu vaccine every year. Why?

Hint

Each antibody only fits one particular microbe. What would happen if the microbe changed shape?

The flu virus changes from year to year. Antibodies only fit the virus they were made for, so old antibodies may not fit the new flu. A new vaccine trains the body for this year's virus.
