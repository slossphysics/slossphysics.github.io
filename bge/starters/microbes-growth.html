Microorganisms and their growth

LI: I am learning what microorganisms need to grow and how their growth can be controlled.

SC: I can name the three main types of microorganism · I can describe what microorganisms need to grow · I can explain how antibiotics and antifungals control growth.

5:00

1.Sort these into bacteria, viruses and fungi.

Drag each one into its group, or tap it and then the group.

Hint

Yeasts, moulds and mushrooms are all fungi. Colds and flu are caused by the smallest microbes of all.

Fungi include yeast and mould. Bacteria include the ones in yoghurt and Salmonella. Colds and flu are caused by viruses.

2.Tap all the things most microorganisms need to grow and multiply. There are 4.

Microorganisms need food, water, warmth and a suitable pH. They don't need light.

3.Milk lasts much longer in the fridge than on the kitchen table. Why?

Microorganisms grow much more slowly when it is cold. The fridge doesn't kill them, which is why food still goes off in the end.

4.A class grows the same bacteria on four plates, each at a different temperature. After 2 days they count the colonies. At which temperature did the bacteria grow best?

The most colonies grew at 37 °C, which is body temperature. At 70 °C there were none: high temperatures kill most bacteria, which is why we cook food thoroughly.

5.In warm conditions, one bacterium can divide into two every 20 minutes. Starting with one bacterium, how many will there be after 2 hours?

That's how many times they divide. Now double from 1 that many times.

You've worked out 6 × 2. But the number doubles each time: 1, 2, 4, …

That's after 5 divisions. How many 20-minute blocks are there in 2 hours?

Hint

First work out how many 20-minute blocks fit into 2 hours. The number of bacteria doubles in each block.

  • 2 hours = 120 minutes, and 120 / 20 = 6 divisions
  • 1 → 2 → 4 → 8 → 16 → 32 → 64
  • 64 bacteria

6.A pupil has a cold and asks their doctor for antibiotics. The doctor says no. Why?

Antibiotics kill or stop the growth of bacteria only. They have no effect on viruses, so they can't cure a cold or flu.

7.Sort these infections: should they be treated with an antibiotic or an antifungal?

Drag each one into its group, or tap it and then the group.

Antibiotics treat infections caused by bacteria. Antifungals treat infections caused by fungi. Despite its name, ringworm isn't a worm.

8.Not all microorganisms are harmful. Tap all the ways microorganisms are useful. There are 3.

Microbes help us make food, digest what we eat, and decompose dead material so its nutrients can be used again. Some do cause harm, but many are useful.

9.Raisins are grapes that have been dried. Why do raisins keep for months, when grapes go mouldy in about a week?

Microorganisms need water. Drying food takes the water away, so microbes can't grow. Salting and adding sugar work in a similar way.

Challenge10.Bacteria are spread over a plate of jelly. Three paper discs, each soaked in a different antibiotic, are placed on it. After 2 days the plate looks like this. What do the results show?

Hint

A clear ring means the antibiotic has spread out into the jelly and stopped the bacteria growing there.

The biggest clear ring is around B, so it stopped the bacteria over the widest area. There is no ring around C, so these bacteria are not affected by it: they may be resistant to that antibiotic.
