BGE · Second and Fourth level · Biological systems · SCN 2-13a, SCN 4-13b

Microbes and enzymes in industry

LI: I am learning how microorganisms and enzymes are used to make useful products.

SC: I can describe how yeast and bacteria are used to make food and drink · I can explain why biological detergents work at low temperatures · I can use results to show what an enzyme does.

5:00

1.Sort these foods and drinks by the microorganism used to make them.

Drag each one into its group, or tap it and then the group.

Hint

Yeast is used when we want bubbles of gas or alcohol. Bacteria are used to turn milk into something thicker and sour.

Yeast (a fungus) makes bread rise and turns sugar into alcohol in beer and wine. Bacteria turn milk into yoghurt and cheese.

2.Bread dough rises because the yeast in it gives off bubbles of gas. Which gas?

Yeast breaks down sugar and gives off carbon dioxide. The bubbles of gas get trapped in the dough and make it rise.

3.A baker wants the yeast in their dough to work well. Tap all the things the yeast needs. There are 3.

Like other microorganisms, yeast needs food (sugar), water and warmth to grow and reproduce. That is why dough is left in a warm place to rise.

4.In brewing, yeast is added to a sugary liquid. The yeast turns the sugar into two products. Tap both.

Yeast turns sugar into alcohol and carbon dioxide. In bread the alcohol is driven off when it is baked. In beer the carbon dioxide gives the fizz.

5.Yoghurt is made by adding bacteria to warm milk. What do the bacteria make that turns the milk thick and sour?

The bacteria feed on the sugar in milk and make an acid. The acid gives yoghurt its sour taste and makes the milk thicken.

6.Biological washing powders contain enzymes. Which statement about them is true?

The enzymes break down stains made from food, grass and blood. Because enzymes work well at low temperatures, clothes can be washed at 30 °C, which saves energy.

7.Pectinase is an enzyme that helps release juice from fruit. A class mashed two 50 g samples of apple and left them for 10 minutes. How much more juice did the sample with pectinase give?

SampleJuice collected (cm³)
Apple and water8
Apple and pectinase21
cm³

You've added the two volumes. The question asks how much more, so find the difference.

That is the total from the pectinase sample. How much more is that than the 8 cm³ without it?

  • Extra juice = 21 − 8
  • Extra juice = 13 cm³
Pectinase breaks down the material that holds plant cells together, so more juice is released.

8.A pupil wants to find out whether pectinase gives more juice from oranges. Which plan is a fair test?

Hint

In a fair test only one thing changes. Which plan changes only whether pectinase is added?

Only one variable changes: pectinase or water. The mass of fruit, the time and the temperature all stay the same. The sample with water is the control to compare against.

Challenge9.A class tested pectinase on 50 g samples of apple at different temperatures. Which conclusion do the results support best?

SampleJuice collected (cm³)
Pectinase at 20 °C13
Pectinase at 40 °C24
Pectinase at 70 °C9
Water only at 40 °C8
Hint

Compare each pectinase result with the water result of 8 cm³. Where does the enzyme make the biggest difference, and where does it hardly help at all?

Pectinase gave the most juice at 40 °C. At 70 °C it gave only 1 cm³ more than water, because high temperatures damage enzymes so they stop working. This is why biological detergents are used in cool washes.
