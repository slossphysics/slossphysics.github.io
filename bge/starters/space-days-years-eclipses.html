Days, years and eclipses

LI: I am learning how the movements of the Earth and Moon give us days, years and eclipses.

SC: I can explain day and night · I can say what a day, a month and a year are · I can describe a solar eclipse and a lunar eclipse.

5:00

1.What causes day and night on Earth?

The Earth spins on its axis once every 24 hours. The side facing the Sun has day; the side facing away has night. The Sun only seems to move across the sky.

2.Match each length of time to what happens in that time.

Drag each time onto what happens, or tap a time and then what happens.

One day (24 hours): the Earth spins once. About one month: the Moon orbits the Earth once. One year (365¼ days): the Earth orbits the Sun once.

3.Sunlight is shining on the Earth from the left. Where is it night?

B is on the half of the Earth facing away from the Sun, so it is night at B. A faces the Sun, so it is day at A.

4.The Earth takes 365¼ days to orbit the Sun. A normal calendar year has 365 days, so every four years the four quarter-days are added together to make a leap year. How many days are in a leap year?

days

That's a normal year. A leap year has the extra quarter-days added on.

That's the number of days in four whole years. How many days are in just the leap year?

Hint

How many quarters make a whole? Add that many days to a normal year.

Four quarter-days make one whole day: 365 + 1 = 366 days. The extra day is 29 February.

5.The Moon looks bright in the night sky. Where does its light come from?

The Moon does not make its own light. We see it because it reflects light from the Sun.

6.Which diagram shows how a solar eclipse happens?

Hint

In a solar eclipse, the Sun is blocked out for people on Earth. Which object would have to get in the way?

In a solar eclipse the Moon passes between the Sun and the Earth, all in a straight line (diagram C). The Moon blocks the Sun's light and its shadow falls on part of the Earth, so the sky goes dark during the day.

7.The Sun, Earth and Moon line up like this. What do people on the night side of the Earth see?

The Earth is between the Sun and the Moon, so the Earth's shadow falls on the Moon. This is a lunar eclipse: the Moon goes dim, often turning a reddish colour.

8.Sort each statement: is it about a solar eclipse or a lunar eclipse?

Drag each card into its group, or tap a card and then the group.

Hint

Solar means to do with the Sun; lunar means to do with the Moon. Ask which one gets hidden.

Solar eclipse: the Moon is between the Sun and the Earth, its shadow falls on the Earth, and the sky goes dark in the daytime. Lunar eclipse: the Earth is between the Sun and the Moon, and the Earth's shadow falls on the Moon, seen from the night side of the Earth.

Challenge9.It is midday in Glasgow (G), so Glasgow faces the Sun. Where will Glasgow be 12 hours later, and why?

Hint

The Earth spins once in 24 hours. What fraction of a turn is 12 hours?

The Earth spins once every 24 hours, so in 12 hours it turns half a turn. Glasgow is carried round to the side facing away from the Sun, so it is midnight.
