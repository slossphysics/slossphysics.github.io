BGE · Second level · Planet Earth · SCN 2-06a

Size and scale of the Universe

LI: I am learning how big things in space are compared with each other.

SC: I can put moons, planets, stars, solar systems, galaxies and the Universe in order of size · I can use a simple scale model of the Sun and Earth.

5:00

1.What is the Sun?

The Sun is a star: a huge ball of hot gas that gives out its own light. It is the closest star to Earth.

2.What is the name of the galaxy we live in?

We live in the Milky Way galaxy. On a very dark night you can see part of it as a faint, milky band across the sky.

3.Put these in order of size, from smallest to largest.

Drag each card to its place, or tap a card and then its place.

Hint

Think about what goes around what, and what is inside what. Something that is inside another thing must be smaller than it.

The Moon orbits a planet (Earth), which orbits a star (the Sun). The Sun and everything orbiting it make up our solar system, which is one of many in the Milky Way galaxy. The Universe holds billions of galaxies.

4.Match each word to its meaning.

Drag each word onto its meaning, or tap a word and then its meaning.

A star makes its own light. A planet orbits a star. A moon orbits a planet. A galaxy is a huge group of billions of stars.

5.Estimate first, then check. About how many times wider than the Earth is the Sun?

6.The Sun is a star. Why does it look so much bigger and brighter than all the other stars in the sky?

The Sun is the closest star to us. The other stars are so much further away that they look like tiny points of light, even though many are bigger and brighter than the Sun.

7.In a scale model, the Sun is a football, about 22 cm wide. The Sun is about 100 times wider than the Earth. Which object would make the best model Earth?

Hint

Divide the width of the football by 100. Remember that 1 cm = 10 mm.

22 cm ÷ 100 = 0.22 cm, which is about 2 mm: a pinhead next to a football.

8.In a scale model, the Sun is a football 22 cm wide. The Earth is about 100 Sun-widths away from the Sun. How far away should the model Earth be?

Hint

Multiply the width of the football by 100, then change centimetres into metres (100 cm = 1 m).

22 cm × 100 = 2200 cm = 22 m. So a football Sun and a pinhead Earth would sit about 22 m apart, across a playground. Space is mostly empty!

Challenge9.The Sun is about 400 times wider than the Moon, but in our sky they look about the same size. What does this tell us?

Hint

Think about a bus far down the road and a ball held at arm's length. How can a big thing look as small as a small thing?

Things look smaller the further away they are. For the much bigger Sun to look the same size as the Moon, it must be much further away: about 400 times further. This is also why the Moon can just cover the Sun in a total solar eclipse.
