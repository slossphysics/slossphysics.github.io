BGE · Fourth level · Planet Earth · SCN 4-06a

Telescopes

LI: I am learning how optical telescopes work and why some are put in space.

SC: I can label a refracting telescope · I can describe how refracting and reflecting telescopes collect light · I can explain the advantages of a telescope in orbit.

5:00

1.What does a refracting telescope use to collect light and bring it to a focus?

A refracting telescope uses lenses, which refract (bend) light. A reflecting telescope uses a curved mirror.

2.This is a refracting telescope. Label the parts marked 1, 2 and 3.

Drag each number onto its name, or tap a number and then its name.

Hint

The objective is nearest the object you are looking at. The eyepiece is where you put your eye.

Light from a distant star (1) enters the large objective lens (2), which collects the light and brings it to a focus. The small eyepiece lens (3) magnifies the image for your eye.

3.Sort each feature: is it true of a refracting telescope, a reflecting telescope, or both?

Drag each card into its group, or tap a card and then the group.

Hint

Refraction is light bending as it passes through something. Reflection is light bouncing off something.

Refracting telescopes gather light with a lens, which bends it. Reflecting telescopes gather light with a curved mirror, which it bounces off. Both use an eyepiece to magnify the image, and in both a bigger lens or mirror collects more light.

4.This is a reflecting telescope. What is the job of the large curved mirror at the back?

The large curved mirror collects the light and reflects it to a focus. A small flat mirror sends the light out of the side of the tube to the eyepiece, which magnifies the image.

5.Why do astronomers build telescopes with very large lenses or mirrors?

A bigger lens or mirror has a bigger area, so it collects more light. That makes the image brighter, so fainter, more distant objects can be seen, in more detail.

6.Sort each statement: is it an advantage of a telescope in space or a telescope on the ground?

Drag each card into its group, or tap a card and then the group.

In space, a telescope is above the atmosphere: no cloud, no twinkling, no light pollution, and it can detect radiation that the atmosphere absorbs. On the ground, telescopes are cheaper, easier to repair and can be much bigger.

7.True or false? The Hubble Space Telescope sees stars without any twinkling because it is closer to them.

Hint

What does starlight have to pass through to reach a telescope on the ground, but not one in space?

False. Twinkling is caused by moving air in the atmosphere bending the starlight by changing amounts. Hubble orbits above the atmosphere, so its images are sharp. Being a few hundred kilometres closer makes no real difference.

8.Many large telescopes on the ground are built on high mountains in dry places, such as Chile and Hawaii. Why? Tap all that apply.

High, dry mountains are above much of the atmosphere (less blurring and absorption), have clear skies with few clouds, and are far from light pollution. It is the next best thing to being in space.

Challenge9.Telescope A has an objective lens 10 cm wide. Telescope B has an objective lens 20 cm wide. How much more light does B collect than A?

Hint

Light is collected over the whole area of the lens, not just across its width.

Light is collected over the lens's area. Doubling the width makes the area 2 × 2 = 4 times bigger (just as four 10 cm squares fit in a 20 cm square), so B collects four times as much light.
