Weight on other planets

Write your answers in your jotter. Use the table to help you, and always say why. Remember: the more mass a planet has, the stronger its gravity.

gGravity on other planets The strength of gravity, g, in newtons per kilogram (N/kg). On Earth, every kilogram is pulled down with 10 N.

AUse the table Everyone. Short answers are fine.

  1. 1What is g on Mars?
  2. 2Which planet has the strongest gravity?
  3. 3Which planet has the same g as Mercury?
  4. 4Is gravity on the Moon stronger or weaker than on Earth?

BWhere would you weigh more? Everyone. Give your answer, then say why.

  1. 1Would you weigh more on Earth or on Mars?
  2. 2Would you weigh more on Venus or on Neptune?
  3. 3Would you weigh more on Jupiter or on Saturn?
  4. 4Put these in order, from where you would weigh the least to where you would weigh the most: Jupiter, the Moon, Earth, Mercury.
  5. 5Name two planets where you would weigh exactly the same.
  6. 6On which planet would it be hardest to lift a heavy box?

CMass or weight? Everyone. Full sentences.

  1. 1An astronaut has a mass of 70 kg on Earth. What is her mass on the Moon? Explain your answer.
  2. 2Ruby says: “You weigh less on the Moon because there is less of you there.” Explain what is wrong with what Ruby says.
  3. 3The Moon is much smaller than the Earth. Explain why its gravity is weaker.
  4. 4You throw a ball straight up as hard as you can, once on Earth and once on the Moon. Where does it go higher? Explain why.

DFinished? Keep going Harder questions for when you have finished A to C.

  1. 1Saturn is much bigger than the Earth, but its gravity is a little weaker (9 N/kg). Suggest why. Hint: what is Saturn made of?
  2. 2A 10 kg dog weighs 40 N on Mars, because W = m × g = 10 × 4. Use W = m × g to work out what the dog would weigh on Jupiter.
  3. 3Tom stands on bathroom scales on Earth, then on the same scales on the Moon. Would the reading go up, go down or stay the same? Explain why.
  4. 4A space probe travels far out into space, a long way from any planet or star. What happens to its mass? What happens to its weight?
