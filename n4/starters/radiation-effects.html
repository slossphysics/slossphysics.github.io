N4 · Waves and Radiation · Nuclear radiation

Effects of nuclear radiation

LI: I am learning how nuclear radiation affects living and non-living things.

SC: I can describe how radiation affects living cells · I can describe its effects on non-living things · I can explain why an object exposed to radiation does not become radioactive.

5:00

1.Sort each effect of nuclear radiation: does it happen to living things or non-living things?

Drag each effect into its group, or tap it and then the group.

In living things, radiation damages cells, which can cause burns and cancers such as leukaemia. In non-living things, it can make materials flash, darken photographic film, and let air carry sparks.

2.Some materials give out a tiny flash of light each time nuclear radiation hits them. What is this effect called?

The flashes are called scintillation. Some radiation detectors count these flashes to measure radiation.

3.How can nuclear radiation cause cancer?

Radiation can damage cells. A damaged cell may die, or it may start to grow and divide out of control, which is cancer.

4.True or false? Hospital syringes are sterilised by passing nuclear radiation through them, so afterwards the syringes are radioactive.

Hint

Something becomes radioactive only if radioactive material gets into it. Does any radioactive material get into the syringes, or does radiation just pass through?

The radiation kills germs as it passes through, but the syringes do not become radioactive. No radioactive material gets into them, so they are safe to use.

5.Workers who use radioactive sources wear a badge that contains photographic film. Why?

Radiation darkens photographic film. The film is checked regularly: the darker it is, the more radiation the worker has received.

6.Two metal plates are connected to a high voltage, with an air gap between them. When a radioactive source is held near the gap, sparks start to jump across it. Why?

Hint

Air normally doesn't conduct electricity. What would change if some of the atoms in the air lost electrons and became charged?

Radiation knocks electrons off atoms in the air, leaving charged particles. Charged particles can move, so the air conducts and sparks can jump.

Challenge7.Nuclear radiation damages cells and can cause cancer. So why do doctors use it to treat cancer?

Hint

Damaging cells is usually bad. When would killing some cells be a good thing?

Radiation damages any cells, so it can be used to kill cancer cells. Doctors aim the beam carefully at the tumour, often from several directions, so the healthy cells around it get as small a dose as possible.
