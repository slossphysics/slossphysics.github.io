Inside the atom

LI: I am learning about the particles that make up an atom.

SC: I can name the three particles in an atom · I can give the charge of each one · I can say where each one is found.

5:00

1.This is a model of an atom. Match each numbered part to its name.

Drag each number onto its name, or tap a number and then its name.

Hint

Look at the charges drawn on each particle. One kind is positive (+), one is negative (−), and one has no charge.

Protons (+) and neutrons (no charge) are packed together in the nucleus at the centre. Electrons (−) move around outside it.

2.Match each particle to its charge.

Drag each particle onto its charge, or tap a particle and then its charge.

Proton: positive. Electron: negative. Neutron: neutral, with no charge (the name gives it away).

3.Where are the electrons in an atom?

Electrons move around outside the nucleus. The nucleus is at the centre.

4.Which particles are found in the nucleus? Tap all that apply.

The nucleus is made of protons and neutrons. Almost all of the atom's mass is in the nucleus.

5.An oxygen atom has 8 protons and 8 neutrons. The atom has no overall charge. How many electrons does it have?

You've added the protons and neutrons. Neutrons have no charge, so only the protons need balancing.

Then the 8 positive protons would have nothing to cancel them out, and the atom would be charged.

Hint

Each electron's negative charge cancels out one proton's positive charge. Neutrons have no charge, so they don't count.

No overall charge means the positive and negative charges balance. 8 protons (+) need 8 electrons (−). In an atom, the number of electrons always equals the number of protons.

6.True or false? Most of an atom is empty space.

The nucleus is tiny compared with the whole atom. If an atom were the size of a football stadium, its nucleus would be about the size of a pea on the centre spot. Almost all the rest is empty space.

7.Some atoms give out nuclear radiation. Which part of the atom does it come from?

Nuclear radiation comes from the nucleus. Some nuclei are unstable: they give out radiation to become more stable.

Challenge8.An atom has 3 protons, 4 neutrons and 3 electrons. It loses one electron. What is its overall charge now?

Hint

Count the positive charges (protons) and the negative charges (electrons) after the electron has gone. Which is bigger?

It now has 3 protons (+) but only 2 electrons (−), so there is one more positive charge than negative: the atom is positive. Nuclear radiation can knock electrons off atoms like this, which is one reason it is harmful.
