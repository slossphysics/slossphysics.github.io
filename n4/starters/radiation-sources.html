N4 · Waves and Radiation · Nuclear radiation

Sources of nuclear radiation

LI: I am learning where nuclear radiation comes from.

SC: I can sort sources into natural and artificial · I can explain what background radiation is · I can name the biggest source of background radiation.

5:00

1.Sort each source of nuclear radiation: natural or artificial (made by people)?

Drag each source into its group, or tap it and then the group.

Hint

For each one, ask: would it still be there if people had never existed?

Natural sources were here long before people: radon, rocks, cosmic rays, and the tiny amounts of radioactive substances in food. Artificial sources are made by people, such as hospital tracers, nuclear waste and plutonium.

2.What is background radiation?

Background radiation is around us all the time, everywhere. It comes from natural sources and from artificial ones.

3.Make a guess: what is the biggest single source of background radiation in the UK?

Radon is a radioactive gas that seeps out of rocks and soil and can build up inside buildings. It gives about half of the background radiation people in the UK receive.

4.True or false? Most background radiation is made by people.

Most background radiation is natural: radon, rocks, cosmic rays and food. It was here long before people built power stations or hospitals.

5.Aberdeen is called the Granite City because many of its buildings are made of granite. Why do people there receive a little more background radiation than people in many other places?

Granite naturally contains small amounts of radioactive substances, so granite buildings and ground add a little to the background radiation.

6.Airline pilots receive more background radiation than people who work on the ground. Why?

Hint

Which natural source of radiation comes from above us? What usually protects us from it on the ground?

The atmosphere absorbs some cosmic rays. High up, there is less air above to block them, so pilots and cabin crew receive more.

7.Your own body is very slightly radioactive. Where does this radiation come from?

Food and drink naturally contain tiny amounts of radioactive substances. Bananas, for example, contain a radioactive form of potassium. These become part of your body.

Challenge8.A pupil says: “If we closed every nuclear power station and hospital, background radiation would drop to zero.” Which reply is right?

Most background radiation is natural: radon from the ground, rocks, cosmic rays and food. Closing power stations and hospitals would hardly change it.
