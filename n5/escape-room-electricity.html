Alert · Power failure

Night shift briefing

Lightning has hit Glen Lochan hydro power station, deep inside the mountain. The whole glen is in darkness, and you're the only engineer on shift. The safety system has sealed every door, and it will only open them for someone who knows their electricity.

  • Every question you get right opens one lock. Open every lock in a room and its door slides open.
  • Each door gives you one letter of the restart code. Collect all nine to switch the power back on.
  • Calculations open in three steps: formula, substitute, solve. Write each line in your jotter as you go. In the exam, each of those lines earns a mark.
  • Stuck on a final answer? After three tries, an emergency override appears. It opens the lock and shows the working, but adds 2 minutes to your time.
  • In the last room you build circuits. Three wrong circuits bring up the override there too.
  • You need a calculator, the relationships sheet and your jotter.

Room 1 of 9 · Input and output devices

Staff entrance

The entrance to the station is locked down. Its security system is built from input and output devices: show it you know which is which.

1.An input device sends a signal into a circuit. An output device changes electrical energy from the circuit into another kind of energy. Sort these devices.

Drag each one into its group, or tap it and then the group.

Input devices (microphone, solar cell, thermistor, LDR) sense something and send a signal into the circuit. Output devices (loudspeaker, motor, LED, buzzer) take electrical energy out of the circuit and do something with it.

2.Match each device to its main energy change.

Drag each device onto its energy change, or tap a device and then the energy change.

Hint

For an input device, electrical energy comes last. For an output device, electrical energy comes first.

The two input devices end with electrical energy: microphone (sound to electrical) and solar cell (light to electrical). The two output devices start with it: motor (electrical to kinetic) and LED (electrical to light).

3.The station needs a warning system that makes a loud sound when the tunnel gets too hot. Which input device and which output device should it use?

A thermistor senses temperature: its resistance changes as it heats up. A buzzer changes electrical energy into sound. Later rooms use the same idea with a transistor in between to do the switching.

Room 2 of 9 · Circuits and meters

Control room

You are in. The lightning has wiped the control panel. Sort out its symbols, signals and meters to bring it back to life.

1.The labels on the control panel have burned off. Match each symbol to its name.

Drag each symbol onto its name, or tap a symbol and then its name.

Hint

Arrows pointing in mean light going in. Arrows pointing out mean light coming out. A thermistor's line has a short flat foot.

An LDR's resistance changes with light level, and a thermistor's changes with temperature. Both turn up in almost every switching circuit in the exam.

2.An oscilloscope is used to test four power supplies. The dashed line on each screen is zero volts. Sort the traces into a.c. and d.c.

Drag each trace into its group, or tap a trace and then the group.

Hint

In d.c. the electrons only ever move one way round the circuit. In a.c. they keep changing direction.

In d.c. the electrons move in one direction only, so the trace stays on one side of zero. In a.c. they change direction again and again, so the trace goes above and below zero. A wave doesn't have to be smooth to be a.c.

3.An engineer wants to measure the current in resistor R and the voltage across resistor R. Two meters, X and Y, are connected as shown. What must each meter be?

An ammeter goes in series with the component, so the same current passes through both. A voltmeter goes in parallel, across the component. When you draw this circuit in the exam, that's one mark for each meter. The variable resistor lets the engineer change the voltage across R.

Room 3 of 9 · Current and voltage rules

Battery room

The backup batteries are flat. Before you reconnect them, check their test circuits using the rules for current and voltage in series and in parallel.

1.Sort each rule into the kind of circuit it describes.

Drag each rule into its group, or tap it and then the group.

Series (one path): the current is the same everywhere, and the voltages add up to the supply voltage. Parallel (branches): the branch currents add up to the supply current, and every branch has the supply voltage across it. These rules are on the relationships sheet.

Hint

In a series circuit the lamps share the supply voltage between them.

The lamps don't have to get equal shares. The two readings must add up to the supply voltage.

That's the supply voltage. In series, the lamps share it between them.

The lamps aren't sharing equally here: the first one has 4.5 V. What is left for the second?

  • Vs = V1 + V2
  • 12 = 4.5 + V2
  • V2 = 7.5 V

3.Two lamps are connected in parallel with a battery. The ammeter beside the battery reads 0.9 A. The ammeter in the top branch reads 0.4 A. What is the reading on the ammeter in the bottom branch?

A
Hint

The current from the battery splits at the junction. Whatever doesn't go along the top branch goes along the bottom one.

You've added the two readings. The 0.9 A from the battery splits between the branches, so the two branch currents add up to 0.9 A.

That would be true in a series circuit. Here the 0.9 A splits at the junction.

The branches don't have to carry the same current. The two branch currents must add up to 0.9 A.

The current isn't splitting equally here: 0.4 A goes along the top branch. What is left for the bottom one?

  • Ip = I1 + I2
  • 0.9 = 0.4 + I2
  • I2 = 0.5 A

Room 5 of 9 · Resistors in series and parallel

Cable gallery

The cables that carry power out of the mountain have tripped. Work out the resistances to reconnect them safely.

1.Two heaters keep the cable gallery dry. They are connected in parallel. An engineer connects a third heater in parallel with them. What happens to the total resistance of the circuit?

Adding a resistor in parallel always decreases the total resistance: there is another path for the current. Adding one in series always increases it.

Room 7 of 9 · Power and energy

Generator hall

The generator is ready, but the station's own equipment has to be checked first. Work out the power, energy and time for each piece of kit.

1.The rating plate on a heater in the generator hall says 2000 W. What does this tell you?

Power is the energy transferred every second. One watt is one joule per second, so a 2000 W heater transfers 2000 J of energy each second it is switched on.

2.Sort these appliances from the station's staff room by their power rating.

Drag each appliance into its group, or tap it and then the group.

Hint

Heating takes a lot of energy every second.

Appliances whose job is to produce heat (kettle, heater, toaster) have high power ratings: they transfer a lot of energy every second. Appliances that produce light or sound, or charge a small battery, need far less.

Room 8 of 9 · Transistor switching and power

Tunnel gate

The tunnel gate is the only way out to the switchyard. Its motor is controlled by a transistor switching circuit: get it working to open the gate.

1.The gate's control board has three components that work as switches. Match each symbol to its name.

Drag each symbol onto its name, or tap a symbol and then its name.

Hint

The relay has a switch above a coil. The npn transistor has an arrow on one slanted leg. The MOSFET has a broken line inside its circle.

Both transistors are switches with no moving parts: they switch on when the voltage at their input is high enough (about 0.7 V for an npn transistor, about 2 V for a MOSFET). A relay is a switch worked by an electromagnet, so a low-voltage circuit can switch on a high-voltage one.

2.The gate motor has a cooling fan that switches on when the motor gets hot. As the temperature of the thermistor increases, its resistance decreases. Explain how the circuit switches on the fan. Put the sentences in order. Two of them are wrong: put those in "Not needed".

Drag each sentence to its place, or tap a sentence and then its place.

Hint

Follow the chain: resistance of the thermistor, then its voltage, then the voltage across the other resistor (which is what the MOSFET sees), then the MOSFET.

In the exam this "explain" is worth 3 marks: the voltage across the thermistor decreases, the voltage across the resistor increases, and the MOSFET switches on. Write it as a chain like this every time, one link per mark.

3.The motor that lifts the tunnel gate has a power of 1.5 kW. It takes 40 s to open the gate. Calculate the energy the motor uses.

  1. Formula

  3. Solve

Check the prefix: 1.5 kW is 1500 W.

You've divided, and the power is still in kW. To get E on its own, multiply both sides by 40.

Room 9 of 9 · Building circuits

Switchyard

You're outside, in the switchyard where the power leaves for the glen. The lightning burned parts out of three test circuits. Finish each circuit to reconnect the grid.

These circuits are live: build on them with your finger.

  • Add a part: drag it from its box onto the circuit. Dropped onto a wire, it joins into that wire.
  • Add a wire: drag from the end of a part to where the wire should go. Or tap the end, then tap where it should go.
  • Change your mind: tap a part or wire you added for Turn and Delete, or use Undo.

1.The switchyard's two warning lamps, P and Q, are connected in parallel. Add an ammeter to measure the current in lamp Q only.

Hint

An ammeter only measures the current that goes through it. Trace lamp Q's path round the circuit with your finger. Which wires carry lamp Q's current and nothing else?

The ammeter is in series with lamp Q, in Q's own branch, so only Q's current goes through it: 0.30 A. In a wire that both lamps share, it would read the total current, 0.60 A.

2.A lamp and a 5 Ω resistor are connected in series with a 3 V battery. Add a voltmeter to measure the voltage across the lamp.

Hint

Leave the circuit as it is. Put the voltmeter in an empty space near the lamp, then draw a wire from each end of the voltmeter to the circuit.

The voltmeter is in parallel with the lamp: one lead joined to each side of it. It reads 1.50 V, the lamp's share of the 3 V supply. Ammeters go in series; voltmeters go in parallel.

3.The yard's floodlight circuit has one lamp left. Add a second lamp so that both lamps are as bright as the first lamp is now. Then add a switch that turns off the new lamp only.

Hint

Two lamps in series have to share the cell's voltage. Put the new lamp in an empty space, then draw a wire from each end of it to the circuit, so it has a path of its own from one side of the cell to the other.

The lamps are in parallel. Each has the cell's full voltage across it, so each is as bright as one lamp on its own. The switch is in the new lamp's branch, so opening it breaks that branch only.
