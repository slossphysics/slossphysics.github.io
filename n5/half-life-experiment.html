N5 · Radiation · Nuclear radiation

Experiment: half-life

LI: I am learning how the activity of a radioactive source changes as time passes.

SC: I can record the activity of a source every 10 seconds · I can draw a graph of activity against time · I can use my graph to find how long the activity takes to halve.

Before you start

A radioactive source is placed in front of a Geiger-Müller tube. The counter shows the activity of the source: the number of nuclei that decay each second, measured in becquerels (Bq).

Take your readings

  1. Press Start. The stopwatch runs for 120 seconds.
  2. Every 10 seconds, the reading to write down appears in the box under the counter. It stays there until the next one.
  3. Watch the source as well. Each dot stands for a nucleus. Nobody can say when any one nucleus will decay: it happens at random.

Draw your graph

  1. Put time (s) along the bottom, from 0 to 120.
  2. Put activity (Bq) up the side, from 0 to 800.
  3. Plot your 13 points, then draw a smooth curve through them. Don't join them with a ruler.

Use your graph

Answer these in your jotter, reading the times from your curve.

  1. How long does the activity take to fall from 800 Bq to 400 Bq?
  2. How long does it take to fall from 400 Bq to 200 Bq?
  3. How long does it take to fall from 200 Bq to 100 Bq?
  4. What do you notice about your three answers? Compare them with the person next to you.
  5. Your readings are not exactly the same as your neighbour's. Why not?
