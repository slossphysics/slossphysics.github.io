Waves

1.Sort each wave into transverse or longitudinal.

Drag each card into its group, or tap a card and then the group.

Hint

Ultrasound is a kind of sound, just too high-pitched for us to hear. Which group does sound go in?

Longitudinal: sound and ultrasound (the particles vibrate along the direction the wave travels). Transverse: water waves and every electromagnetic wave, such as light and microwaves (vibrations at right angles to the direction of travel).

2.A loudspeaker makes a sound of frequency 500 Hz in air. The speed of sound in air is 340 m s⁻¹. Calculate the wavelength of the sound.

m

You've multiplied. Rearrange v = fλ to get λ on its own.

You've divided the frequency by the speed. Rearrange v = fλ to get λ on its own.

  • v = fλ
  • 340 = 500 × λ
  • λ = 0.68 m

3.Which part of the electromagnetic spectrum has a longer wavelength than visible light, but a shorter wavelength than microwaves?

In order of decreasing wavelength (increasing frequency): radio, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays, gamma rays.

4.A ray of light passes from air into a glass block at an angle. What happens to the light as it enters the glass?

Light slows down in glass, so its wavelength gets shorter and it bends towards the normal. The frequency stays the same.

5.A house in a hilly area gets good long-wave radio reception but poor TV reception from the same transmitter. Why?

Long waves diffract more than short waves. Long-wave radio bends round the hills into the valley; the shorter-wavelength TV signals do not bend as much.

Radiation

6.Sort each fact into alpha, beta or gamma.

Drag each card into its group, or tap a card and then the group.

Alpha is a helium nucleus: big, slow and the most ionising, so it is stopped by paper. Beta is a fast electron, stopped by a few mm of aluminium. Gamma is an EM wave: the least ionising, so it is the most penetrating.

7.A worker's hand absorbs a dose of 0.40 mGy from fast neutrons. The radiation weighting factor for fast neutrons is 10. Calculate the equivalent dose.

mSv

You've divided. Equivalent dose is absorbed dose multiplied by the weighting factor.

That's the answer in Sv. The unit box says mSv.

  • H = DwR
  • H = 0.40 × 10
  • H = 4.0 mSv

8.The activity of a radioactive source falls from 800 kBq to 100 kBq in 36 hours. What is its half-life?

hours

You've divided 36 by 8. Count how many times the activity halves to get from 800 down to 100.

That's just half of 36 hours. Count how many times the activity halves to get from 800 down to 100.

The activity doesn't halve four times. Write it out: 800, then half of that, and so on, until you reach 100.

Hint

Half-life is the time for the activity to halve. Start at 800 and keep halving. How many halvings to reach 100?

  • 800 → 400 → 200 → 100 kBq is 3 half-lives
  • half-life = 36 ÷ 3
  • half-life = 12 hours

Power, energy and time

9.Spot the mistake. A 2.0 kW kettle is switched on for 3 minutes. A pupil calculates the energy it transfers:

Change to SI units first: 2.0 kW = 2000 W and 3 minutes = 180 s.
  • E = Pt
  • E = 2000 × 180
  • E = 360 000 J

10.A 60 W lamp transfers 216 kJ of energy. Calculate how long it was switched on for.

s

Change kJ to J first: 216 kJ = 216 000 J.

You've multiplied. Rearrange P = E / t to get t on its own.

You've multiplied. Rearrange P = E / t to get t on its own.

You've divided the power by the energy. Rearrange P = E / t to get t on its own.

  • P = E / t
  • 60 = 216 000 / t
  • t = 3600 s
That's exactly 1 hour.

Dynamics

11.Sort each quantity into vector or scalar.

Drag each card into its group, or tap a card and then the group.

Vectors: displacement, velocity, acceleration and force. Scalars: distance, speed, mass, time and energy. Speed and distance are the scalar partners of velocity and displacement.

12.A pupil walks 40 m north, then 30 m east. What is the size of their displacement?

Hint

The two walks make a right angle. Displacement is the dashed line from start to finish: which rule finds the long side of a right-angled triangle?

  • s² = 40² + 30²
  • s² = 2500
  • s = 50 m
Displacement is a vector, so in the exam give its direction too: tan θ = 30 / 40, so θ = 37°, a bearing of 037.

13.The velocity–time graph shows a car's motion. Calculate its displacement in the 10 s shown.

m

That's 12 × 10, as if the car did 12 m s⁻¹ the whole time. Split the area into a triangle and a rectangle.

That's only the rectangle, from 4 s to 10 s. Add on the triangle for the first 4 s.

That's only the triangle, for the first 4 s. Add on the rectangle from 4 s to 10 s.

That's the acceleration in the first 4 s (the gradient). Displacement is the area under the line.

Hint

Displacement is the area under a velocity–time graph. Split it into shapes you can find the area of.

  • s = area under the graph
  • s = (½ × 4 × 12) + (6 × 12)
  • s = 24 + 72 = 96 m

14.A cyclist slows down from 9.0 m s⁻¹ to 3.0 m s⁻¹ in 4.0 s. Calculate the cyclist's acceleration.

m s⁻²

Nearly. v is the final velocity, so v − u = 3.0 − 9.0. The cyclist is slowing down, so the answer is negative: type a minus sign.

You've added the velocities. Acceleration is the change in velocity, v − u, divided by time.

That's just the final velocity divided by the time. Use the change in velocity, v − u.

  • a = (v − u) / t
  • a = (3.0 − 9.0) / 4.0
  • a = −1.5 m s⁻²
The minus sign shows the cyclist is slowing down (decelerating).

15.A car of mass 1200 kg has the forces shown acting on it. Calculate its acceleration.

m s⁻²

You've only used the driving force. Find the unbalanced force first.

You've added the forces. They act in opposite directions, so find the difference.

That's only the 600 N force divided by the mass. Find the unbalanced force first.

Hint

In F = ma, F is the unbalanced force. The two forces push in opposite directions.

  • F = 3000 − 600 = 2400 N
  • F = ma
  • 2400 = 1200 × a
  • a = 2.0 m s⁻²

16.A ball of mass 0.50 kg is dropped from a height of 5.0 m. Assuming no energy is lost, calculate its speed just before it hits the ground.

m s⁻¹

That's the potential energy it loses, in J. Now use Ek = ½mv² to find its speed.

That's v². Take the square root.

Don't forget the ½ in Ek = ½mv².

Don't forget the ½ in Ek = ½mv², and take the square root at the end.

Hint

The Ep it loses on the way down all becomes Ek. Find Ep first.

  • Ep = mgh = 0.50 × 9.8 × 5.0 = 24.5 J
  • Ek = ½mv²
  • 24.5 = ½ × 0.50 × v²
  • v = 9.9 m s⁻¹

17.Ball A is dropped from a table. At the same moment, ball B is pushed horizontally off the same table. Ignore air resistance. Which ball lands first?

Horizontal and vertical motion are independent. Both balls have the same constant vertical acceleration, so they take the same time to fall. Ball B also has a constant horizontal velocity, so it lands further away.

Space

18.Which statement about a geostationary satellite is true?

A geostationary satellite has a period of 24 hours at an altitude of 36 000 km, so it stays above the same point on the Earth. A satellite in a higher orbit has a longer period.

19.Proxima Centauri is 4.2 light-years from Earth. What is this distance in metres?

Hint

A light-year is the distance light travels in one year. Use d = vt, with v = 3.0 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹ and t in seconds.

  • t = 4.2 × 365.25 × 24 × 60 × 60 = 1.33 × 10⁸ s
  • d = vt
  • d = 3.0 × 10⁸ × 1.33 × 10⁸
  • d = 4.0 × 10¹⁶ m

20.The line spectrum from a star is shown with the line spectra of four elements. Which elements are in the star? Tap all that apply.

Hint

Run your finger straight up from each of an element's lines. Is there a line in the same place in the star's spectrum?

An element is in the star only if every one of its lines appears in the star's spectrum. Every line of A and C matches; B has only one matching line.

Challenge21.A space probe of mass 800 kg is landing on Mars, where g = 3.7 N kg⁻¹. Its engine pushes upwards with a force of 4000 N. Calculate the size of the probe's acceleration.

m s⁻²

That's the engine force divided by the mass. The probe's weight acts downwards too.

You've added the weight to the engine force. They act in opposite directions.

You've used g on Earth (9.8 N kg⁻¹). The probe is on Mars.

You've used g on Earth (9.8 N kg⁻¹). The probe is on Mars.

Hint

Two forces act on the probe: the engine force up and its weight down. Find its weight first.

  • W = mg = 800 × 3.7 = 2960 N
  • F = 4000 − 2960 = 1040 N (upwards)
  • F = ma
  • 1040 = 800 × a
  • a = 1.3 m s⁻²
The unbalanced force is upwards, so if the probe is falling it slows down for a gentle landing.
