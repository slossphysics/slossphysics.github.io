Equivalent dose rate

LI: I am learning to calculate equivalent dose rate and compare doses with the safety limits.

SC: I can use Ḣ = H/t to find a dose rate, a dose or a time · I can state the annual dose limits · I can decide whether a dose is within a limit.

1.What does equivalent dose rate tell you?

Equivalent dose rate is the equivalent dose per unit time. Its unit depends on the time used: Sv s⁻¹, mSv h⁻¹ or mSv y⁻¹, for example.

Hint

The limits are for doses on top of background radiation. People who choose to work with radiation, and are monitored, are allowed a higher limit than everyone else.

In the UK the average annual background radiation is 2.2 mSv. On top of that, the annual limit is 1 mSv for a member of the public and 20 mSv for a radiation worker.

5.An engineer at a nuclear power station receives an equivalent dose of 12 mSv from her work in one year. The annual limits are 1 mSv for the public and 20 mSv for radiation workers. Which statement is correct?

She is a radiation worker, so her limit is 20 mSv a year. 12 mSv is within it, although it is far more than a member of the public is allowed.
