Nuclear fission recap

LI: I am recalling what I learned about nuclear fission and nuclear power stations.

SC: I can say what nuclear fission is · I can describe the role of neutrons in a chain reaction · I can put the parts of a nuclear power station in order · I can write my answers in my jotter.

8:00

1.What is meant by the term nuclear fission? Write your answer in your jotter first, then tap the best match.

In nuclear fission, a large nucleus splits into two smaller nuclei, releasing energy and 2 or 3 neutrons. Always say nucleus: “an atom splits” gets no mark in the exam.

2.Which particle is fired at a uranium-235 nucleus to make it split?

A neutron. It has no charge, so the positive protons in the nucleus do not push it away.

3.Describe the role of neutrons in a fission chain reaction. Write your answer in your jotter first (it is worth 2 marks), then tap the answer that would get both marks.

One mark for the neutrons released go on to split other nuclei, and one for chain reaction (the process keeps repeating). Tick your own answer for each mark.

4.Put the parts of a nuclear power station in order, from the fuel to the electricity.

Drag each part into its place, or tap a part and then its place.

Reactor → boiler → turbine → generator. Fission in the reactor makes heat, the heat boils water to make steam, the steam spins the turbine, and the turbine turns the generator.

5.In a nuclear power station, what actually spins the turbine?

Steam. Heat from fission boils water, and the steam spins the turbine, exactly like a coal or gas power station.

Challenge6.Tap the statements about fission in a nuclear power station that are correct. There are 2.

A neutron splits the uranium nucleus, and the neutrons released cause further fission: a chain reaction. The reactor only makes heat; the generator makes the electricity.
