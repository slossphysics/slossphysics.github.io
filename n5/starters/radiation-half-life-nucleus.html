N5 · Radiation · Nuclear radiation

Half-life and the nucleus

LI: I am recalling half-life and radiation before we learn about nuclear fission.

SC: I can set out a half-life calculation with arrows · I can say what is meant by half-life · I can name the particles in the nucleus · I can set out my working in my jotter.

5:00

1.Iodine-131 is used to treat thyroid cancer. Its half-life is 8 days. A sample has an activity of 640 Bq. Calculate its activity after 24 days.

Bq

You've divided by 3. The activity halves three times: halve it, halve the answer, then halve again.

That's the activity after one half-life. How many half-lives are there in 24 days?

That's the activity after two half-lives. How many lots of 8 days fit into 24 days?

That's four halvings. How many lots of 8 days fit into 24 days?

You've divided the activity by the time. Work out the number of half-lives first, then halve that many times.

The activity of a source goes down as time passes, not up.

Hint

Start with the number of half-lives. How many lots of 8 days fit into 24 days?

Another hint

24 ÷ 8 = 3 half-lives. Write 640 Bq in your jotter and draw three arrows after it.

Another hint

Halve at each arrow: 640 Bq, then 320 Bq, then keep going until you have used all three arrows.

Number of half-lives = 24 ÷ 8 = 3

  • 640 Bq → 320 Bq → 160 Bq → 80 Bq
  • Activity after 24 days = 80 Bq

2.The activity of a radioactive source falls from 1200 Bq to 150 Bq in 30 minutes. Calculate its half-life.

minutes

That's the number of half-lives. Now share the 30 minutes between them.

That's half of the time. The half-life is the time for the activity to halve once: keep halving 1200 Bq until you reach 150 Bq, and count the arrows.

1200 ÷ 150 = 8 is not the number of half-lives. Keep halving 1200 until you reach 150, and count the arrows.

You've counted four half-lives. Keep halving 1200 until you reach 150, and count the arrows again.

You've multiplied. The 30 minutes is shared between the half-lives.

That's the activity at the end, in becquerels. The question asks for a time.

Hint

This one works backwards. Write 1200 Bq and keep halving until you reach 150 Bq.

Another hint

1200 Bq, then 600 Bq, then 300 Bq, then 150 Bq. Count the arrows: that is the number of half-lives.

Another hint

Those half-lives took 30 minutes altogether. Share the 30 minutes equally between them.

  • 1200 Bq → 600 Bq → 300 Bq → 150 Bq
  • 3 half-lives = 30 minutes
  • Half-life = 30 ÷ 3 = 10 minutes

3.What is meant by the term half-life?

Half-life is the time taken for the activity of a source to fall to half (or for half of the radioactive nuclei to decay). In the exam, “the radiation halves” does not get the mark.

4.Which type of nuclear radiation is stopped by a sheet of paper?

Alpha is stopped by a sheet of paper (or a few centimetres of air). Beta is stopped by a few millimetres of aluminium, and gamma is reduced by thick lead.

5.Tap the particles that are found in the nucleus of an atom. There are 2.

The nucleus is made of protons and neutrons. Electrons are outside the nucleus. We need the nucleus today: in nuclear fission, a nucleus splits in two.

Challenge6.Some radioactive waste from a nuclear power station has a half-life of 32 years. Calculate how long it takes for the activity of the waste to fall to one eighth of its starting value.

years

That's the number of half-lives. How long do they take altogether?

You've divided the half-life by 8. Each halving takes 32 years: how many halvings take the activity to one eighth?

You've multiplied by 8. How many times do you halve to get to one eighth?

That's two half-lives, which takes the activity to one quarter. Keep halving until you reach one eighth.

That's four half-lives, which takes the activity to one sixteenth. Count the halvings again.

That's half of the half-life. How many half-lives does it take to reach one eighth?

Hint

Start with 1 and keep halving: how many halvings until you reach one eighth?

Another hint

1, then ½, then ¼, then ⅛. Count the halvings: each one takes 32 years.

  • 1 → ½ → ¼ → ⅛: 3 half-lives
  • Time = 3 × 32 = 96 years
