N5 · Radiation · Nuclear radiation

Radiation recall

LI: I am recalling what I know about nuclear radiation before we start half-life.

SC: I can describe alpha, beta and gamma radiation · I can say what ionisation is · I can calculate absorbed dose, equivalent dose and equivalent dose rate.

8:00

1.Match each description to the type of radiation.

Drag each description onto its radiation, or tap a description and then its radiation.

Alpha is a helium nucleus (2 protons and 2 neutrons). Beta is a fast-moving electron. Gamma is an electromagnetic wave, so it has no mass and no charge.

2.Match each type of radiation to the material needed to absorb it.

Drag each radiation onto its absorber, or tap a radiation and then its absorber.

Hint

The biggest particle is the easiest to stop. The one with no mass and no charge gets through the most.

Alpha is absorbed by a sheet of paper (or a few centimetres of air). Beta is absorbed by a few millimetres of aluminium. Gamma is the most penetrating: it takes several centimetres of lead to absorb most of it.

3.What happens to a neutral atom when it is ionised?

Ionisation is when a neutral atom gains or loses an electron. The atom is left with a charge and is called an ion. This is how radiation damages living cells.

4.Which type of radiation causes the least ionisation as it passes through a material?

Gamma causes the least ionisation, which is why it travels furthest through materials. Alpha causes the most ionisation, so it is absorbed most easily.
