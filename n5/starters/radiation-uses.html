Uses of nuclear radiation

LI: I am recalling how nuclear radiation is used in medicine and industry, before we start half-life.

SC: I can give medical and industrial uses of radiation · I can explain which type of radiation suits a tracer and a thickness gauge · I can calculate an equivalent dose rate.

8:00

1.Sort each use of nuclear radiation: medical or industrial?

Drag each use into its group, or tap it and then the group.

Medical uses diagnose or treat patients, or keep equipment free of germs. Industrial uses help to make, check and power things.

2.A radioactive tracer is injected into a patient. A detector outside the body follows where it goes. Which type of radiation should the tracer give out, and why?

Hint

The detector is outside the body. Which radiation is penetrating enough to get out through the patient's tissue?

A tracer gives out gamma radiation. It is penetrating enough to pass out of the body to the detector, and it causes the least ionisation on the way, so it does the least harm to the patient.

3.A paper mill checks the thickness of its paper with this gauge. Radiation from the source passes through the paper to the detector. Which type of radiation should the source give out?

Hint

The gauge only works if some of the radiation gets through the paper, and the amount changes when the thickness changes. Think about what a sheet of paper does to each type.

Beta: the paper absorbs some of it, and how much gets through depends on the thickness. Alpha would all be absorbed, and nearly all the gamma would pass through whatever the thickness.

4.A factory rolls aluminium foil and checks it with this gauge, which uses a beta source. The detector reading suddenly goes up. What has happened to the foil?

Thinner foil absorbs less beta radiation, so more reaches the detector and the reading goes up. The rollers then move apart to make the foil thicker again.

5.How is nuclear radiation used to treat a cancer tumour?

Radiation ionises atoms in living cells, and a high enough dose kills the cells. In radiotherapy, beams are aimed at the tumour from several directions, so the tumour receives a high dose and the healthy tissue around it receives a much lower one.

Challenge8.The half-life of a source is the time taken for its activity to halve. A hospital needs a tracer for a scan that takes about an hour. Which source is the most suitable?

Hint

Two things to get right. The radiation has to get out of the body to the detector. And the tracer has to last long enough for the scan, without staying active in the patient for longer than it needs to.

Gamma can pass out of the body to the detector. A half-life of 6 hours is long enough for the scan, and short enough that the activity soon falls away, which keeps the patient's dose small.
